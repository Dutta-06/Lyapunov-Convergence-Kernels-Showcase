% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
This is a complete mathematical treatment of adaptive tracking control with Gaussian radial-basis-function
(RBF) networks whose weights, centres and widths are adapted online. Part~I is the projection-based adaptive RBF
controller of version~1. An audit found its stability argument to be a correct but standard composition of known
results, with two claims wrong as written (a non-convex ``projection'' for centre separation and an
implicit-function-theorem argument that is not a proof); it contains no new theorem. We state it with explicit
assumptions, a Hessian-free disturbance bound and an RMS bound, and list the errata to version~1. Part~II develops
transport adaptive control: the network is an empirical measure on a lifted parameter space, and the adaptive law is
its transport. Theorems A--F, C$'$ and D$'$ are proved; A, B, C, C$'$, E and F are also checked by tests.
Theorem~E is self-contained: an explicit strict Lyapunov function (Lemma E.0) gives the radius, overshoot and rate of
the label-free convergence under tangential excitation. Theorem~C$'$ is the OT $\sigma$-modification toward a prior
with a different number of atoms, through the barycentric projection of the optimal plan (Lloyd's algorithm in
continuous time); Theorem~D$'$ is propagation of chaos for the dithered closed loop, at the dimension-free rate
$m^{-1/2}$. Proposition~G is formal and checked directly. Theorem~H shows that the
averaged mean-field loop with saturated features, $\sigma$-modification and dither is mean-field Langevin dynamics and
converges globally and exponentially to the regularised optimum; this is proved for the averaged system, the
reduction to it is formal, and the real loop is a conjecture. At finite gain the averaged loop is in general not a
gradient flow of any free energy in the $\WG$ metric: Proposition~H.7 (frozen-measure part proved) exhibits a
semi-gradient term, whose performative defect is non-reciprocal in the checked instances ($n = 1, 2$), and an
orbit-area circulation kernel ($n \ge 2$). With a second-order averaging term they predict a closed-loop effect for
which a targeted literature search found no precedent: traversing the reference orbit backwards changes the learned
kernels by $O(1/k_e)$, although the occupation measure is unchanged. At $k_e = 40$ the prediction matches to 3\% in
size and 11\% as a vector (cosine 0.994). Proposition~H.8, proved assuming the chain rule for the perturbed flow,
gives input-to-state stability of the averaged system. Theorem~I characterises the non-reciprocity at order
$k_e^{-2}$: for $n = 1$ the averaged loop is reciprocal exactly when the residual vanishes on the orbit, and no open
set of measures is reciprocal for any $n$. Theorem~J proves the deterministic finite-$m$ case of the averaging
conjecture: every hyperbolic critical point of the regularised loss has a unique nearby periodic orbit of the real
closed loop with the same stability type, whose period mean is the first-order prediction up to $O(k_e^{-2})$.
Proposition~K shows that a collapsed configuration is a strict local minimum iff the leak gain exceeds a critical
value $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and that in a window around $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ the direction in which the
reference is traversed decides whether the kernels separate. A second increment adds the sharp slow-regime rate
(Lemma~E.0$'$), uniform-in-time propagation of chaos in the leak-dominated regime (Theorem~D$''$) and the stationary
law of the dithered finite-size loop with its non-equilibrium current (Theorem~J$'$, Proposition~J$'$.2). A third
increment adds the explicit stationary parameter spread of the dithered linearised loop (Corollary~E.0$'$.3) and
the slow-regime Lyapunov function for non-periodic persistently exciting regressors (Lemma~E.0$''$) and
uniform-in-time propagation of chaos in the learning regime for the averaged slow loop (Theorem~D$'''$, with the
finite-$m$ weight-sensitivity law of Proposition~D$'''$.5, the real loop on a warm start of Corollary~D$'''$.7, the
leak's prior as a design input of the window, Proposition~D$'''$.9, and the barycentric OT leak's mass constraint,
which removes the finite-size floor, Proposition~D$'''$.12 --- in the real loop too, where the collapsed subspace
carries one $K_0$-atom orbit for every commensurate size, Corollary~D$'''$.14 and Proposition~D$'''$.15, and
Theorem~J holds uniformly in the number of kernels in the per-particle norm, Theorem~D$'''$.18, while a soft
(entropic) mass constraint equalises the masses only down to a critical excess, Proposition~D$'''$.20; and the
mass-constrained energy satisfies a Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz inequality whose constant does not depend on the number
of kernels, which gives a measure-level basin with an exponential rate and, at the nearest-atom leak, explains the
floor as a spurious minimum at every admissible count vector, Theorem~D$'''$.21). A fourth increment adds adaptive
priors, whose learned atoms remove a misplaced prior's bias and, at every representable sample, the finite-size floor
(Theorem~D$'''$.23, Proposition~D$'''$.24) --- a property of the amplitude--mass gauge that survives an approximation
error, Proposition~D$'''$.28, with a projection that restores the leak's a-priori bound, Proposition~D$'''$.27 ---,
perfect tracking in the real loop with a Floquet spectrum that does not depend on the number of kernels
(Corollary~D$'''$.26, Lemma~D$'''$.26a), second-order plants and a cogging-torque servo (Proposition~D$'''$.29),
measurement noise, which is coherent across the kernels and high-passed by the loop, and against which the desired
regressor bounds the noise's own persistent force and keeps the replicated kernels together
(Propositions~D$'''$.30 and D$'''$.31, Corollary~D$'''$.33), the cohesion leak, a leak on the unexcited subspace
without a prior, which does not select the number of kernels (Proposition~D$'''$.32), a replicator weight law,
the mass transfer of the cohesion leak (Proposition~D$'''$.34), the split of a class, an $S_n$-symmetric
transcritical bifurcation that sheds the extreme particle (Proposition~D$'''$.35), the gauge under dither, along
which the replicator's masses diffuse and which an entropic leak on the masses pins (Proposition~D$'''$.36), and the
dither's drift along it, the replicator flow of a log-determinant free energy that drives the masses into the
saturation (Proposition~D$'''$.37).
\end{abstract}
