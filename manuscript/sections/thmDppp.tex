\section{Theorem D\texorpdfstring{$'''$}{'''}: uniform-in-time propagation of chaos in the learning regime (the
averaged slow loop)}\label{sec:thmDppp}

Theorem D$''$ ends where the leak stops dominating the loop gain: in the learning regime its small-gain argument has
nothing to say, and the long-horizon run of \code{exp_chaos_uniform.py} showed the coupled distance growing over the
horizon. This section isolates the structure that the small-gain argument throws away. Under the synchronous coupling
the interaction between the particles enters through the fitted function $\fhat_\Theta$, which is linear in the
empirical measure; the loss $\bar L$ is a convex quadratic of it (flat convexity, Theorem H(i)); and the interaction
term of the coupling identity is therefore the negative of a squared $L^2(\rho)$-norm at linear order --- it
contracts, whatever the gains. What remains and can expand is the self term, whose curvature is the Hessian of the
first-variation potential, $\sigma_0\Ginv - R[\mu](\vartheta)$ with $R$ the \emph{residual-weighted} curvature of the
features --- the same object as Proposition K's $R_2$ at a collapsed configuration. Proposition K says that the
collapsed configuration is unstable when this curvature exceeds the leak \emph{with the collapsed residual}; at a
fitted configuration the residual is the approximation error, and the second-order optimality condition of the limit
measure says that the curvature is \emph{below} the leak at every atom. The theorem below turns this into a
uniform-in-time statement for the averaged slow loop at $\tau = 0$, with explicit constants, in the regime in which
the network learns.

\paragraph{Setting.} The averaged slow dynamics of Section~\ref{sec:thmJprime} at $\tau_0 = 0$ (Theorem J$'$'s
leading order $\bar L_0$ without dither; slow time $s = t/k_e$, leak $\sigma_0 = k_e\sigma$, $f = f(x)$ continuous,
$\rho$ the occupation measure of the $T_0$-periodic reference, $F_\rho := \sup_{\supp\rho}\norm{f}$, saturated
features, $K_\Phi := \sup\norm{\tilde\Phi}$), written as the $G$-preconditioned gradient flow of
$J_m(\Theta) = m\bar L(\mu_\Theta) + \frac{\sigma_0}2\sum_i\norm{\vartheta_i - \vartheta^0}^2_{\Ginv}$:
\begin{gather}
  \frac{\dd\vartheta_i}{\dd s} = -G\nabla_{\vartheta_i}J_m(\Theta) = -G\nabla_\vartheta\mathcal{V}[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i),\qquad
  i = 1..m,
  \tag{$\mathfrak{A}_m$}\\
  \mathcal{V}[\mu](\vartheta) := V_L[\mu](\vartheta) + \frac{\sigma_0}2\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}^2_{\Ginv},\notag
\end{gather}
with $V_L[\mu](\vartheta) = -\langle r_\mu^\T\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta)\rangle_\rho$ and $r_\mu = f - \fhat[\mu]$,
$\fhat[\mu] := \int\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta')\dd\mu(\vartheta')$, as in Section~\ref{sec:thmH}. Its mean-field
equation is the Wasserstein gradient flow $\Aavg$ of Section~\ref{sec:thmH} at $\tau = 0$ in slow time,
$\partial_s\mu_s = \nabla\cdot(\mu_sG\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_s])$, solved by the characteristic flow
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd X_s(\vartheta)}{\dd s} = -G\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_s](X_s(\vartheta)),\qquad X_0 = \mathrm{id},\qquad
  \mu_s = (X_s)_\#\mu_0
  \tag{$\mathfrak{A}_0$}
\end{equation}
(a McKean--Vlasov equation without noise whose vector field has linear growth through the leak, is Lipschitz in
$\vartheta$ and, through $r_\mu$, Lipschitz in $\mu$ for $W_1$; existence, uniqueness and the representation by
characteristics follow from Dobrushin's argument~\cite{S35} --- the synchronous-coupling proof of Theorem D$'$(b)
with the plant removed --- and the velocity is bounded on the invariant ball of Step~0). The constants are taken in
the $\Ginv$ metric on parameters (``whitened''):
\[
  L_\vartheta^G := \sup_{x,\vartheta}\norm{\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta)G^{1/2}},\qquad
  L_2^G := \sup_{x,\vartheta,\norm{u}=1}\norm{G^{1/2}\nabla^2_\vartheta\bigl(u^\T\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta)\bigr)G^{1/2}},
\]
so that $\norm{\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta) - \tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta')}\le L_\vartheta^G\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta'}_{\Ginv}$,
$\norm{\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta)v}\le L_\vartheta^G\norm{v}_{\Ginv}$ and
$\norm{(\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta) - \nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta'))v}
\le L_2^G\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta'}_{\Ginv}\norm{v}_{\Ginv}$ ($L_\vartheta^G\le\lambda_G^{1/2}L_\vartheta$ and
$L_2^G\le\lambda_GL_2$ in the constants of Section~\ref{sec:thmDprime};
\code{chaos_learning.whitened_lipschitz_constants} computes the suprema by search, with closed-form bounds). The
\emph{residual-weighted curvature} of $\mu$ at $\vartheta$ is the symmetric matrix
\begin{gather*}
  R[\mu](\vartheta) := \Bigl\langle\sum_kr_\mu^k(x)\nabla^2_\vartheta\tilde\Phi^k(x;\vartheta)\Bigr\rangle_\rho
  = -\nabla^2_\vartheta V_L[\mu](\vartheta),\qquad R^G := G^{1/2}RG^{1/2},\\
  \kappa_R[\mu](A) := \sup_{\vartheta\in A}\lambda_{\max}\bigl(R^G[\mu](\vartheta)\bigr),
\end{gather*}
so that $G^{1/2}\nabla^2_\vartheta\mathcal{V}[\mu](\vartheta)G^{1/2} = \sigma_0I - R^G[\mu](\vartheta)$; for
$\mu = \delta_{\bar\vartheta}$ and $\vartheta = \bar\vartheta$ it is Proposition K's $R_2$ and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\bar\vartheta) = \kappa_R[\delta_{\bar\vartheta}](\{\bar\vartheta\})$. It satisfies
$\norm{R^G[\mu](\vartheta)}\le L_2^G\langle\norm{r_\mu}\rangle_\rho\le L_2^G(F_\rho + K_\Phi)$ and
$\norm{R^G[\mu](\vartheta) - R^G[\nu](\vartheta)}\le L_2^G\norm{\fhat[\mu] - \fhat[\nu]}_{L^1(\rho)}$.
$N_\varrho(A) := \{\vartheta : \operatorname{dist}_{\Ginv}(\vartheta,A)\le\varrho\}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{The coupling.} $\vartheta_i(0)$, $i = 1..m$, i.i.d.\ with law $\mu_0$ of compact support,
$R_0 := \sup_{\supp\mu_0}\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}$; $\Theta(s)$ solves \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m$)} and
the reference particles are $\bar\vartheta_i(s) := X_s(\vartheta_i(0))$, i.i.d.\ with law $\mu_s$ and
$\bar\vartheta_i(s)\in\supp\mu_s$. Let $\delta_i := \vartheta_i - \bar\vartheta_i$,
\[
  D(s) := \frac1m\sum_i\norm{\delta_i(s)}^2_{\Ginv},\qquad
  Y(s) := \norm{\fhat_{\bar\Theta(s)} - \fhat[\mu_s]}_{L^2(\rho)},
\]
$Y$ being the sampling fluctuation of the reference's fitted function
($\E Y(s)^2 = \frac1m\langle\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_s}\tilde\Phi(x;\cdot)\rangle_\rho\le K_\Phi^2/m$). A priori
(Step~0 below) every particle of either system stays in the ball
$\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}\le R_\Theta := \max\{R_0,\ L_\vartheta^G(F_\rho + K_\Phi)/\sigma_0\}$, so
$D\le D_{\max} := 4R_\Theta^2$, and the characteristics move at speed at most
$V_{\max} := L_\vartheta^G(F_\rho + K_\Phi) + \sigma_0R_\Theta$.

\begin{thm}{Theorem}{D$'''$}[uniform-in-time propagation of chaos in the learning regime, $\tau = 0$]
Assume
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=3em]
  \item[\textup{(H)}] \emph{(non-degenerate stability along the mean-field flow)} there are $s_1\ge0$, $\kappa > 0$
    and $\varrho' > 0$ such that
    \[
      \kappa_R[\mu_s]\bigl(N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_s)\bigr)\le\sigma_0 - \kappa\qquad\text{for every }s\ge s_1,
    \]
\end{itemize}
and put $a(s) := \sigma_0 - \kappa_R[\mu_s](N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_s))$ (so $a\ge\kappa$ on $[s_1,\infty)$ and
$a\ge\sigma_0 - L_2^G(F_\rho + K_\Phi)$ everywhere) and $\Lambda_1 := \int_0^{s_1}(\kappa - a(s))_+\dd s < \infty$.
Define
\begin{gather*}
  \varrho := \min\bigl\{(2\kappa)^{1/2}/L_2^G,\ \varrho'\kappa e^{-\Lambda_1}/(4(L_\vartheta^G)^2)\bigr\},\\
  \theta := \min\bigl\{\kappa/(8L_2^G),\ \varrho\kappa e^{-\Lambda_1}/(4L_\vartheta^G),\ \varrho'\kappa e^{-\Lambda_1}/(4L_\vartheta^G)\bigr\},\\
  \Delta := \theta/(4L_\vartheta^GV_{\max}),\qquad C_1 := 4e^{2\Lambda_1}(L_\vartheta^G)^2K_\Phi^2/\kappa^2,\qquad
  m_0 := 16K_\Phi^2/\theta^2.
\end{gather*}
Then for every $m\ge m_0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item \emph{(exponentially long horizons)} for every $S > 0$ and every $s\in[0,S]$,
    \[
      \E D(s)\le C_1/m + 4R_\Theta^2(S/\Delta + 2)\exp\bigl(-m\theta^2/(32K_\Phi^2)\bigr);
    \]
    in particular $\sup_{s\le S}\E D(s)\le(C_1 + 12R_\Theta^2)/m$ for all $S\le\Delta e^{m\theta^2/(64K_\Phi^2)}$ and
    $m\ge m_0$ with $e^{m\theta^2/(64K_\Phi^2)}\ge m$.
  \item \emph{(uniform in time)} if moreover \textup{(H$'$)} the characteristic flow converges exponentially on the
    support of $\mu_0$, $\sup_{\vartheta\in\supp\mu_0}\norm{X_s(\vartheta) - X_\infty(\vartheta)}_{\Ginv}\le C_2e^{-\kappa_2s}$
    for all $s\ge0$, then for every $s\ge0$
    \[
      \E D(s)\le C_1/m + 4R_\Theta^2(R_{\mathrm{eff}}/\Delta + 3)\exp\bigl(-m\theta^2/(32K_\Phi^2)\bigr),\qquad
      R_{\mathrm{eff}} := \kappa_2^{-1}\bigl(\log(8L_\vartheta^GC_2/\theta)\bigr)_+.
    \]
  \item \emph{(the learned function and the particles)} in either case, with the same right-hand side $B(s)$ for
    $\E D(s)$,
    \begin{gather*}
      \E\norm{\fhat_{\Theta(s)} - \fhat[\mu_s]}^2_{L^2(\rho)}\le2(L_\vartheta^G)^2B(s) + 2K_\Phi^2/m,\\
      \E W_2(\mu_{\Theta(s)},\mu_s)\le B(s)^{1/2} + \E W_2(\mu_{\bar\Theta(s)},\mu_s),
    \end{gather*}
    and on the event $\mathcal{G}_S := \{\sup_{s\le S}Y(s)\le\theta\}$ (case \textup{(a)}; $\mathcal{G}_\infty$ in
    case \textup{(b)}), whose complement is the tail above, every particle stays within $3\varrho'/4$ of its reference
    up to time $S$: $\max_i\sup_{s\le S}\norm{\delta_i(s)}_{\Ginv}\le3\varrho'/4$ --- no particle changes basin.
\end{enumerate}
The constants depend on $(\kappa,\varrho',\Lambda_1,L_\vartheta^G,L_2^G,K_\Phi,F_\rho,\sigma_0,R_0)$ and not on $m$,
$s$ or $S$.
\end{thm}
\status{Proved (synchronous coupling with the three-term identity of Step~1, a bootstrap on the mean square and the
per-particle distance, and McDiarmid's inequality~\cite{S36} for the reference's sampling fluctuation; the
finite-horizon part of the transient is Theorem D$'$'s mechanism and appears in $\Lambda_1$). Hypothesis (H) is a
property of the deterministic mean-field flow and can be computed; it is the strict second-order optimality condition
of the limit configuration with a margin and a neighbourhood (Remark D$'''$.1), and it is compatible with the learning
regime $\sigma_0 < \sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ of Proposition K. It requires the support of $\mu_s$ to stay away from the
ridges of $\mathcal{V}[\mu_s]$ for $s\ge s_1$: for an initial cloud whose support is a union of pieces each flowing
into one basin (a warm start, or a cluster per bump) this holds; for a connected cold start whose mass splits between
several basins it fails for every $s$ (Remark D$'''$.2), and the numerics below show what the coupling does there.
Numerics: \code{exp_chaos_learning.py} $\to$ \code{results/chaos/learning_table.md} (below). The theorem is about the
averaged slow dynamics; its transfer to the real loop at finite $k_e$ is open (Remark D$'''$.4). Its constants are
worst-case and its regime is asymptotic: on the instance below $m_0$ is of order $10^{23}$ (Remark D$'''$.6), and the
finite-$m$ behaviour is described sharply by Proposition D$'''$.5. Referee round 8
(\code{review/REVIEW_round8_Dppp.md}) re-derived every constant of Steps 0--9, found no counterexample to (2.1),
(3.1), (4.1) on an adversarial search, and corrected the wording of Remarks D$'''$.1--D$'''$.2, the good event per
horizon and the well-posedness sentence (all applied). Referee round 9 (\code{review/REVIEW_round9_D3b.md}) reviewed
Proposition D$'''$.5, Corollary D$'''$.7 and the numerics: all hold with fixes, the main one being that the finite-$m$
replicated configuration is a minimiser only for admissible weights (Proposition D$'''$.5(iv)) --- one of the four
$m = 16$ warm seeds sits on a saddle, which the numerics paragraph now says. Novelty (literature audit): the technique (synchronous
coupling + bootstrap + bounded differences) is standard; the sign structure of the interaction and the local,
residual-weighted hypothesis --- and with them a uniform-in-time statement in a regime where the network learns ---
are new in propagation of chaos for adaptive control; the theorem's regime is asymptotic in $m$ and the finite-$m$ law
is a delta-method statement, new in its object.}

\paragraph{Literature audit} (\code{LITERATURE_TRANSPORT.md}, D$'''$).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Prior.} Uniform-in-time propagation of chaos by synchronous coupling under \emph{global} convexity of
    the confinement and convexity of the interaction is Malrieu's~\cite{S37}; non-convex confinements with
    \emph{small} Lipschitz interactions are handled by reflection coupling~\cite{S38}; flat-convex energies with dither
    by the entropy method~\cite{S5}. The Hessian decomposition ``Gram + residual-weighted feature curvature'' is the
    standard tool of the interpolation\,/\,PL-condition literature~\cite{S39} and of lazy-training analyses, where the
    second term vanishes at interpolation.
  \item \emph{Distinction.} (i) The interaction of the fitted network is neither small nor convex in the particle
    coordinates; its sign comes from the flat convexity of $\bar L$ ($\fhat$ linear in the measure), which makes
    $-\norm{\fhat_\Theta - \fhat_{\bar\Theta}}^2$ the leading interaction term under the coupling --- the finite-$m$,
    two-measure version of \cite{S39}'s decomposition. (ii) The convexity needed is local:
    $\sigma_0I - G^{1/2}R[\mu_s]G^{1/2}\succeq\kappa I$ on a neighbourhood of the support of the mean-field flow,
    i.e.\ the strict second-order optimality condition of the fitted configuration, evaluated at the actual residual
    --- not a global convexity (which would force the collapsed configuration to be stable and the network not to
    learn, Theorem D$''$). (iii) The window $\kappa_R[\mu_\infty] < \sigma_0 < \sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ between the
    collapsed instability (Proposition K) and the fitted stability is a design statement about the leak that has no
    counterpart in the cited work. (iv) The limits are stated with the result: a connected initial support that splits
    between basins violates the hypothesis for all times (the characteristic flow is a homeomorphism), and the
    coupling then has a floor of pairs in different basins; the real loop at finite $k_e$ is not covered.
  \item \emph{Proposition D$'''$.5 (the weight-sensitivity law).} At finite $m$ the stationary coupled distance is the
    multinomial fluctuation of the cluster weights propagated through the implicit function $w\mapsto\Theta^*(w)$: a
    delta-method statement (the Gaussian limit of a smooth function of a multinomial), standard in statistics; what is
    specific is its object (the shared-particle coupling of a particle approximation to a stationary $K$-atom measure,
    with the covariance $(1/m - 1/M)(\operatorname{diag}\bar w - \bar w\bar w^\T)$) and its role as the sharp form of
    the theorem's constant (stiffness$^{-1}$ $\times$ feature correlations against $\kappa^{-1}L_\vartheta^GK_\Phi$).
  \item \emph{Proposition D$'''$.9 (measure-valued priors).} Leaks toward a single prior ($\sigma$-modification,
    Ioannou--Kokotovi\'c) are the standard of robust adaptive control; regularisation toward the nearest of several
    centres is standard in machine learning (mixture priors, prototype regularisers) and this document's own
    Theorem C$'$ leaks toward a prior \emph{measure} through an optimal plan. The nearest-atom leak is the simplest
    measure-valued prior; what is new is its role in the window of Theorem D$'''$ --- the prior's placement sets the
    stationary residual, hence the lower end of the window and the relaxation rate --- and the finding that removing
    the bias raises the coupling's floors (on the instance the weight sensitivity rises with the amplitudes): a
    bias--variance trade-off stated for the particle system. Verdict: standard object, new design statement; the
    function-level sensitivity law is the delta method once more.
  \item \emph{Proposition D$'''$.12 (the mass constraint).} Capacity-constrained and equal-mass quantisation
    through optimal plans, and semi-discrete optimal transport with its Laguerre cells, are classical
    (\cite{S19,S42}); the integrality of the transportation polytope and the forest structure of its vertices are
    textbook (\cite{S40}); $c$-cyclical monotonicity is the standard optimality criterion (\cite{S41}). What is
    new is the observation that the finite-$m$ floor of the mean-field limit of an adaptive law is carried by a
    conservation law of its leak (the cluster masses under the nearest-atom leak), and that a design of the leak
    --- the mass constraint of the OT plan --- breaks it: on the commensurate sizes the stationary measure of the
    particle system coincides with the mean-field one, and the finite-$m$ fluctuation of the plan is counted (at
    most $K_0 - 1$ split particles) rather than estimated. Propagation of chaos with an exactly $m$-independent
    stationary law is not in the literature we know of; the mean-field form of the barycentric leak for an atomic
    prior (the Laguerre-cell leak) was Section~\ref{sec:program}'s open item. Numerics: \code{exp_ot_leak.py}.
    Verdict: textbook ingredients, new mechanism and new object (a conservation law of the leak as the source of
    the finite-size floor, broken by the plan's mass constraint).
  \item \emph{Proposition D$'''$.17 (convergence of the OT dynamics).} The Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz route to the
    convergence of (sub)gradient trajectories of definable functions is standard (\cite{S43,S44}); its application to
    an adaptive law whose energy is a semiconcave transport cost plus a smooth loss is a routine verification (the
    saturated Gaussian features are exponential-analytic, the plan is a minimum over finitely many vertices).
    Verdict: imported tool, routine verification; the content is that the mass-constrained law converges without a
    rate, which separates the qualitative question (settled) from Theorem D$'''$'s quantitative one (open for the OT
    dynamics).
\end{itemize}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 0 (a priori bounds).} For either system,
$\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}^2_{\Ginv}
= 2\ip{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}{\langle\nabla_\vartheta(r^\T\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta))\rangle_\rho}
- 2\sigma_0\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}^2_{\Ginv}$ with
$\abs{\ip{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}{\langle\nabla_\vartheta(r^\T\tilde\Phi)\rangle_\rho}}
= \abs{\langle r^\T\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\vartheta - \vartheta^0)\rangle_\rho}
\le\langle\norm{r}\rangle_\rho L_\vartheta^G\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}
\le(F_\rho + K_\Phi)L_\vartheta^G\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}$, so
$D^+\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}\le L_\vartheta^G(F_\rho + K_\Phi) - \sigma_0\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}$
and $\norm{\vartheta(s) - \vartheta^0}_{\Ginv}\le R_\Theta$; hence $\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}\le2R_\Theta$,
$D\le D_{\max}$, and
$\norm{\dd\vartheta/\dd s}_{\Ginv} = \norm{G^{1/2}\langle\nabla_\vartheta(r^\T\tilde\Phi)\rangle_\rho - \sigma_0G^{-1/2}(\vartheta - \vartheta^0)}\le V_{\max}$.
Two Lipschitz facts: for $\Theta$, $\bar\Theta$ with rows $\vartheta_j$, $\bar\vartheta_j$ and
$A_j(x) := \int_0^1\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x;\bar\vartheta_j + t\delta_j)\dd t$,
\begin{gather}
  \fhat_\Theta - \fhat_{\bar\Theta} = \frac1m\sum_jA_j\delta_j =: h,\qquad
  \norm{h}_{L^2(\rho)}\le L_\vartheta^G\frac1m\sum_j\norm{\delta_j}_{\Ginv}\le L_\vartheta^GD^{1/2},
  \tag{0.1}\\
  \begin{split}
  &\norm{R^G[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\vartheta) - R^G[\mu_s](\vartheta)}\le L_2^G\norm{r_{\bar\Theta} - r_{\mu_s}}_{L^1(\rho)}\le L_2^GY,\\
  &\norm{(\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta_i) - A_i(x))v}\le\frac{L_2^G}2\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}\norm{v}_{\Ginv}.
  \end{split}
  \tag{0.2}
\end{gather}

\smallskip
\emph{Step 1 (the identity).} Since $\dd\delta_i/\dd s = -G[\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i)
- \nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_s](\bar\vartheta_i)]$ and $\ip{\delta}{Gv}_{\Ginv} = \ip{\delta}{v}$,
\begin{gather*}
  \tfrac12\frac{\dd D}{\dd s} = -\frac1m\sum_i\ip{\delta_i}{\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i) - \nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_s](\bar\vartheta_i)}
  = T_A + T_B + T_C,\\
  T_A := -\frac1m\sum_i\ip{\delta_i}{\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\vartheta_i) - \nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\bar\vartheta_i)},\\
  T_B := -\frac1m\sum_i\ip{\delta_i}{\nabla V_L[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i) - \nabla V_L[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\vartheta_i)},\\
  T_C := -\frac1m\sum_i\ip{\delta_i}{\nabla V_L[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\bar\vartheta_i) - \nabla V_L[\mu_s](\bar\vartheta_i)}
\end{gather*}
(the leak is linear and sits in $T_A$; the three brackets telescope to
$\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i) - \nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_s](\bar\vartheta_i)$). The self term $T_A$
compares the \emph{reference's} potential at the two points; the interaction term $T_B$ compares the two empirical
measures at the $m$-system's point; $T_C$ is the reference's sampling error.
\code{chaos_learning.AveragedSlowLoop.decomposition} evaluates the three terms and checks the identity to rounding.

\smallskip
\emph{Step 2 (the self term is local convexity).}
$\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\vartheta_i) - \nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\bar\vartheta_i)
= \int_0^1\nabla^2\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\bar\vartheta_i + t\delta_i)\dd t\cdot\delta_i
= \sigma_0\Ginv\delta_i - \bar R_i\delta_i$ with $\bar R_i := \int_0^1R[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\bar\vartheta_i + t\delta_i)\dd t$,
so $T_A = -\sigma_0D + \frac1m\sum_i\ip{\delta_i}{\bar R_i\delta_i}$ and
$\ip{\delta}{R\delta}\le\lambda_{\max}(R^G)\norm{\delta}^2_{\Ginv}$. If $\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}\le\varrho'$ the
segment lies in $N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_s)$ ($\bar\vartheta_i\in\supp\mu_s$), and by (0.2)
$\lambda_{\max}(R^G[\mu_{\bar\Theta}])\le\kappa_R[\mu_s](N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_s)) + L_2^GY$ there. Hence, as long
as $\max_i\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}\le\varrho'$,
\begin{equation}
  T_A\le-\bigl(a(s) - L_2^GY(s)\bigr)D.
  \tag{2.1}
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\emph{Step 3 (the interaction term is a negative square at linear order).}
\[
  \nabla V_L[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i) - \nabla V_L[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\vartheta_i)
  = \langle\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_i)^\T(\fhat_\Theta - \fhat_{\bar\Theta})\rangle_\rho
  = \langle\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_i)^\T h\rangle_\rho,
\]
so, with the $L^2(\rho;\R^n)$ inner product $\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  T_B &= -\Bigl\langle\Bigl\langle\frac1m\sum_i\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_i)\delta_i,\,h\Bigr\rangle\Bigr\rangle
  = -\norm{h}^2 - \Bigl\langle\Bigl\langle\frac1m\sum_i(\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_i) - A_i)\delta_i,\,h\Bigr\rangle\Bigr\rangle\\
  &\le-\norm{h}^2 + \norm{h}\frac{L_2^G}2D\le\frac{(L_2^G)^2D^2}{16}
  \end{split}
  \tag{3.1}
\end{equation}
by (0.2) and $\max_x(-x^2 + cx) = c^2/4$. The leading part
$-\norm{h}^2 = -\norm{\fhat_\Theta - \fhat_{\bar\Theta}}^2_{L^2(\rho)}$ is the flat convexity of $\bar L$: the
interaction contracts, and only the Taylor remainder of the features, quadratic in $D$, is charged. This is the step
that Theorem D$''$'s small-gain bound replaces by $+L_\vartheta^2D$-type terms.

\smallskip
\emph{Step 4 (the sampling term).}
$\nabla V_L[\mu_{\bar\Theta}](\bar\vartheta_i) - \nabla V_L[\mu_s](\bar\vartheta_i)
= \langle\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\bar\vartheta_i)^\T(\fhat_{\bar\Theta} - \fhat[\mu_s])\rangle_\rho$, so
$\abs{\ip{\delta_i}{\cdot}}\le L_\vartheta^G\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}Y$ and
\begin{equation}
  T_C\le L_\vartheta^GD^{1/2}Y.
  \tag{4.1}
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\emph{Step 5 (the differential inequalities).} While $\max_i\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}\le\varrho'$,
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12\frac{\dd D}{\dd s}\le-(a - L_2^GY)D + \frac{(L_2^G)^2D^2}{16} + L_\vartheta^GD^{1/2}Y,
  \tag{5.1}
\end{equation}
and per particle, from the same three brackets with
$\abs{T_{B,i}}\le\norm{\delta_i}L_\vartheta^G\norm{h}\le\norm{\delta_i}(L_\vartheta^G)^2D^{1/2}$ by (0.1) and
$\abs{T_{C,i}}\le\norm{\delta_i}L_\vartheta^GY$,
\begin{equation}
  D^+\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}\le-(a - L_2^GY)\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv} + (L_\vartheta^G)^2D^{1/2} + L_\vartheta^GY.
  \tag{5.2}
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\emph{Step 6 (the bootstrap on the good event).} Fix a horizon $S\le\infty$, let
$\mathcal{G}_S := \{\sup_{s\le S}Y(s)\le\theta\}$ and
$T^* := \inf\{s\le S : D(s)\ge\varrho^2\text{ or }\max_i\norm{\delta_i(s)}_{\Ginv}\ge\varrho'\}$ ($T^* > 0$ since
$\delta(0) = 0$; everything below is on $[0,S]$). On $\mathcal{G}_S$ and $[0,T^*)$: $L_2^GY\le\kappa/8$,
$(L_2^G)^2D/16\le(L_2^G)^2\varrho^2/16\le\kappa/8$, and
$L_\vartheta^GD^{1/2}Y\le(\kappa/8)D + (2(L_\vartheta^G)^2/\kappa)Y^2$ (Young), so (5.1) gives
$\dd D/\dd s\le-2(a - 3\kappa/8)D + (4(L_\vartheta^G)^2/\kappa)Y^2$. Since
$a - 3\kappa/8\ge\kappa/2 - (\kappa - a)_+$, $\int_r^s(a - 3\kappa/8)\ge(\kappa/2)(s - r) - \Lambda_1$ for $r\le s$,
and Gr\"onwall gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  D(s)\le Z(s) &:= 4e^{2\Lambda_1}(L_\vartheta^G)^2\kappa^{-1}\int_0^se^{-\kappa(s-r)}Y(r)^2\dd r\\
  &\le4e^{2\Lambda_1}(L_\vartheta^G)^2\kappa^{-2}\theta^2\le\varrho^2/4\qquad\text{on }\mathcal{G}_S\cap[0,T^*)
  \end{split}
  \tag{6.1}
\end{equation}
by the second constraint on $\theta$. Per particle, (5.2) with $D^{1/2}\le\varrho/2$ and $Y\le\theta$, and
$\int_r^s(a - \kappa/8)\ge(\kappa/2)(s - r) - \Lambda_1$, gives
$\norm{\delta_i(s)}_{\Ginv}\le2e^{\Lambda_1}\kappa^{-1}[(L_\vartheta^G)^2\varrho/2 + L_\vartheta^G\theta]
\le\varrho'/4 + \varrho'/2 = 3\varrho'/4$ by the second constraint on $\varrho$ and the third on $\theta$. Both
thresholds are therefore strict on $[0,T^*)$, so $T^* = S$ on $\mathcal{G}_S$ by continuity: $D\le Z$ and
$\max_i\norm{\delta_i}\le3\varrho'/4$ on $[0,S]$ on $\mathcal{G}_S$. $Z$ does not involve the $m$-system.

\smallskip
\emph{Step 7 (expectation).} For $s\le S$,
\begin{multline*}
  \E D(s)\le\E[Z(s)] + D_{\max}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_S^c)
  \le4e^{2\Lambda_1}(L_\vartheta^G)^2\kappa^{-1}\int_0^se^{-\kappa(s-r)}\E Y(r)^2\dd r + D_{\max}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_S^c)\\
  \le C_1/m + 4R_\Theta^2\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_S^c),
\end{multline*}
using $\E Y(r)^2\le K_\Phi^2/m$.

\smallskip
\emph{Step 8 (the good event).} $Y(s)$ is a function of the i.i.d.\ sample $(\vartheta_j(0))_{j\le m}$:
$Y(s) = \norm{\frac1m\sum_j[\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;X_s(\vartheta_j(0))) - \int\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;X_s(\vartheta))\dd\mu_0]}_{L^2(\rho)}$.
Changing one $\vartheta_j(0)$ changes it by at most $2K_\Phi/m$ (bounded differences), and
$\E Y(s)\le(\E Y^2)^{1/2}\le K_\Phi/m^{1/2}$; McDiarmid's inequality~\cite{S36} gives
$\mathbb{P}(Y(s)\ge K_\Phi/m^{1/2} + t)\le\exp(-mt^2/(2K_\Phi^2))$, so for $m\ge m_0 = 16K_\Phi^2/\theta^2$
($K_\Phi/m^{1/2}\le\theta/4$, $t = \theta/4$)
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}(Y(s)\ge\theta/2)\le\exp\bigl(-m\theta^2/(32K_\Phi^2)\bigr)\qquad\text{for every fixed }s.
  \tag{8.1}
\end{equation}
In time, $\abs{Y(s) - Y(s')}\le2L_\vartheta^GV_{\max}\abs{s - s'}$ (each $X_s(\vartheta)$ moves at speed
$\le V_{\max}$ and $\tilde\Phi$ is $L_\vartheta^G$-Lipschitz, for the sample and for the mean), so on a grid of step
$\Delta = \theta/(4L_\vartheta^GV_{\max})$, $Y\le\theta$ on $[s_k,s_k + \Delta]$ whenever $Y(s_k)\le\theta/2$. (a) The
grid $\{k\Delta : k\Delta\le S\}\cup\{S\}$ has at most $S/\Delta + 2$ points, and a union bound with (8.1) gives
$\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_S^c) = \mathbb{P}(\sup_{s\le S}Y > \theta)\le(S/\Delta + 2)\exp(-m\theta^2/(32K_\Phi^2))$;
with Step~7 this is (a). For $S\le\Delta e^{m\theta^2/(64K_\Phi^2)}$,
$(S/\Delta + 2)e^{-m\theta^2/(32K_\Phi^2)}\le3e^{-m\theta^2/(64K_\Phi^2)}\le3/m$ when
$e^{m\theta^2/(64K_\Phi^2)}\ge m$. (b) Under (H$'$), for $s\ge R_{\mathrm{eff}}$ every $X_s(\vartheta)$ is within
$\theta/(8L_\vartheta^G)$ of $X_\infty(\vartheta)$ on $\supp\mu_0$, so $Y(s)\le Y(\infty) + \theta/4$ with
$Y(\infty) := \norm{\frac1m\sum_j[\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;X_\infty(\vartheta_j(0))) - \int\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;X_\infty)\dd\mu_0]}$,
another bounded-differences statistic with $\E Y(\infty)\le K_\Phi/m^{1/2}$; the grid on $[0,R_{\mathrm{eff}}]$ plus
the point at infinity has at most $R_{\mathrm{eff}}/\Delta + 3$ points, and
$\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_\infty^c) = \mathbb{P}(\sup_{s\ge0}Y > \theta)\le(R_{\mathrm{eff}}/\Delta + 3)\exp(-m\theta^2/(32K_\Phi^2))$.

\smallskip
\emph{Step 9 (the learned function and the measures).}
$\norm{\fhat_\Theta - \fhat[\mu_s]}\le\norm{h} + Y\le L_\vartheta^GD^{1/2} + Y$ by (0.1), so
$\E\norm{\cdot}^2\le2(L_\vartheta^G)^2\E D + 2K_\Phi^2/m$. The coupling $(\vartheta_i,\bar\vartheta_i)$ is a coupling
of $\mu_\Theta$ and $\mu_{\bar\Theta}$, so $W_2^2(\mu_\Theta,\mu_{\bar\Theta})\le D$ ($\Ginv$ metric), and the
triangle inequality gives (c). The per-particle statement is Step~6.
\end{proof}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.1}[what the hypothesis says, and the window]
(H) at $s = \infty$ reads: on a neighbourhood of every atom of the limit measure,
$G^{1/2}\nabla^2\mathcal{V}[\mu_\infty]G^{1/2} = \sigma_0I - R^G[\mu_\infty]\succeq\kappa I$ --- each atom is a
non-degenerate local minimiser of the first-variation potential $\mathcal{V}[\mu_\infty]$, with margin $\kappa$: the
strict form of the second-order optimality condition for measures (the local Chizat--Bach condition, as in
Proposition K(ii)). Its non-strict form, $\sigma_0\Ginv - R[\mu_\infty](\vartheta^*)\succeq0$ at every atom
$\vartheta^*$ of positive mass, is necessary for an atomic stationary measure to be a local minimiser of $\mathcal{G}$
in the Wasserstein sense: splitting an atom of mass $w$ along a direction of negative curvature of
$\mathcal{V}[\mu_\infty]$ lowers $\mathcal{G}$ at second order (Proposition K(i)--(ii) with the atom's own residual).
Along the flow, (H) asks for this with the \emph{current} residual $r_{\mu_s}$ from the time $s_1$ on. It does not
mention the collapsed configuration: $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}} = \kappa_R[\delta_{\bar\vartheta}](\{\bar\vartheta\})$
is the same curvature \emph{with the collapsed residual}, which is the residual of the best single kernel and is
large when the plant needs several. The learning regime $\sigma_0 < \sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ (the kernels separate)
and the uniform regime (H) ($\kappa_R[\mu_\infty]\le\sigma_0 - \kappa$ at the fitted configuration) therefore coexist
exactly when the fitted configuration is non-degenerately stable while the collapsed one is not --- the window
\[
  \kappa_R[\mu_\infty]\bigl(N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_\infty)\bigr) < \sigma_0 < \sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\bar\vartheta)
\]
of leaks in which the network learns \emph{and} propagation of chaos is uniform in time. Theorem D$''$'s condition,
by contrast, asks the leak to dominate the worst-case residual curvature $\lambda_GL_2(F_\rho + K_\Phi)$ \emph{and}
the interaction's Lipschitz constant $L_\vartheta^2$-type terms, which forces $\sigma_0\ge\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$.
The two ingredients that remove the horizon here are (i) the sign of the interaction, which is the flat convexity of
$\bar L$ (Step~3), and (ii) the residual-weighted curvature evaluated at the actual residual rather than at the worst
case (Step~2). Both are properties of the lifted linear parametrisation $\fhat = \int\tilde\Phi\dd\mu$, not of the
particular features.
\end{rmk}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.2}[the transient, the support, and the floor of the coupling]
Two things limit the theorem, and both are visible in the numerics. (i) The constant carries $e^{2\Lambda_1}$, the
finite-horizon amplification of Theorem D$'$ over the time the flow needs to enter the non-degenerate regime: for a
warm start $\Lambda_1 = 0$ and $C_1 = 4(L_\vartheta^G)^2K_\Phi^2/\kappa^2$; for a cold start the expanding phase
($a(s) < \kappa$ while the residual is large) lasts a time of order $1/\sigma_0$ and $\Lambda_1$ is of order
$\sigma_0^{-1}\cdot\sup(\kappa_R - \sigma_0)$ --- the bound is uniform but quantitatively void, as D$'$'s $e^{\Lambda T}$
is. (ii) (H) is a statement about the $\varrho'$-neighbourhood of the \emph{support} of $\mu_s$. The characteristic
flow is a homeomorphism, so a connected support stays connected: if the mass of a connected cold start splits between
several basins of $\mathcal{V}[\mu_\infty]$, the support of $\mu_s$ threads through the ridges between them for every
$s$, and the filament's points converge to the saddles of $\mathcal{V}[\mu_\infty]$ on those ridges, where
$\mathcal{V}$ has a direction of negative curvature and $\kappa_R > \sigma_0$ --- (H) fails for every $s_1$
(asymptotically in $s$; pointwise the filament may well lie in a locally convex region for a while, referee round 8,
D3-11), however little mass the filament carries. The theorem then says nothing, and the coupling can indeed carry a
floor: a particle whose reference lies near a separatrix can be pushed to the other side by the $O(m^{-1/2})$
difference between the two drifts, after which the pair sits in different basins for ever, at a distance of the order
of the basin separation. A fraction $p_m$ of such pairs contributes $p_m\cdot O(R_\Theta^2)$ to $D$, so $D$ follows
the law of $p_m$ (the $\mu_0$-mass of a boundary layer of width $O(m^{-1/2})$ around the separatrices' preimage, hence
$p_m = O(m^{-1/2})$ when $\mu_0$ has a bounded density there), not $1/m$; the learned function is less sensitive,
$\norm{\fhat_\Theta - \fhat_{\bar\Theta}}\le L_\vartheta^G\frac1m\sum\norm{\delta_i}_{\Ginv}
\le L_\vartheta^G(p_m\cdot2R_\Theta + D_{\mathrm{good}}^{1/2})$, which is still $O(m^{-1/2})$. The measure-weighted
form of (H) --- a bad set of small $\mu_s$-mass, with the per-particle bootstrap run on the good particles only and
the interaction split as in Step~3 --- is the natural extension and is listed in Section~\ref{sec:program}; what the
present theorem shows is where the clean statement holds (supports inside basins) and why the cold start of Theorem
D$''$'s experiment was outside it on two counts (the transient and the connected support). (iii) The floor has a
second source at small $m$, on a warm start: when the realised cluster weights fall outside the admissible set of
Proposition D$'''$.5(iv) the replicated configuration is a saddle of $J_m$, the $m$-system lingers on it and then
splits a cluster, so that pairs end in different basins although the mean-field flow satisfies (H); its probability
is exponentially small in $m$ but appreciable at $m = 16$ on the instance below.
\end{rmk}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.3}[dither]
At $\tau_0 > 0$ the synchronous coupling cancels the noise and the identity of Step~1 holds pathwise with the same
three terms, but the reference law has full support and the particles visit the ridges of $\mathcal{V}$ with the
Gibbs weight of the ridge, so (H) fails in the sense of (ii) above and the coupling carries a floor of that order.
Uniform-in-time propagation of chaos at $\tau_0 > 0$ is the province of the entropy method: with dither,
\textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m$)} is the $m$-particle mean-field Langevin dynamics of the free energy $F_\tau$ of Theorem H
in the $\Ginv$ metric (Corollary J$'$.3 is its stationary statement), and \cite{S5} proves uniform-in-time
propagation of chaos for mean-field Langevin dynamics under flat convexity, regularity of the first variation and a
uniform log-Sobolev inequality for the proximal Gibbs measures --- the three hypotheses that Theorem H(i)--(ii)
verifies for $F_\tau$, with the constant $\rho_\tau$ of H(ii). That result is imported here, not re-derived; its
constants are those of Theorem H and are exponentially small in $1/\tau_0$, where the constants of Theorem D$'''$
are those of the leak and the residual curvature. The leak-dominated regime is covered for the real loop and every
$\tau\ge0$ by Theorem D$''$.
\end{rmk}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.4}[the real loop]
Theorem D$'''$ is a statement about the averaged slow dynamics \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m$)}. Theorems J and J$'$
identify \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m$)} as the leading order of the real loop's slow dynamics, with the corrections
$\varepsilon\cdot b$ (Theorem J$'$'s $b$, not a gradient: Proposition J$'$.2) and the fast error eliminated at
$O(\varepsilon^2)$; the transfer of a uniform-in-time statement through these corrections --- a near-identity change
of variables that is uniform in time --- is the open item of Section~\ref{sec:program}, branch 1; on a warm start it
is settled by Corollary D$'''$.7 below through Theorem J. What the theorem settles is the mechanism: in the learning
regime the uniformity rests on the fitted configuration's non-degenerate stability and on the flat convexity of the
loss, not on a dominant leak; the real-loop run on a warm start (numerics, table 5) tracks the averaged slow
dynamics over the horizon run, at the same $k_e$ at which Theorem D$''$'s cold start grew.
\end{rmk}

\begin{thm}{Proposition}{D$'''$.5}[the stationary coupled distance at finite $m$: the weight-sensitivity law]
Let $\mu^* = \sum_{k=1}^K\bar w_k\delta_{\vartheta^*_k}$ be a $K$-atom stationary measure of
\textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0$)} (each $\vartheta^*_k$ a critical point of $\mathcal{V}[\mu^*]$), and for weights
$w\in(0,\infty)^K$ let $\mathcal{G}_w(\Theta) := \frac12\langle\norm{f - \sum_kw_k\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_k)}^2\rangle_\rho
+ \frac{\sigma_0}2\sum_kw_k\norm{\vartheta_k - \vartheta^0}^2_{\Ginv}$ be the mean-field energy of
$\mu_w := \sum_kw_k\delta_{\vartheta_k}$, so that $\nabla_{\vartheta_k}\mathcal{G}_w(\Theta) = w_k\nabla_\vartheta\mathcal{V}[\mu_w](\vartheta_k)$.
Assume the $K$-atom Hessian $\nabla^2_\Theta\mathcal{G}_{\bar w}(\Theta^*)$ is positive definite. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \emph{(the weighted stationary point and its replication)} there is a smooth map $w\mapsto\Theta^*(w)$ on a
    neighbourhood of $\bar w$ with $\Theta^*(\bar w) = \Theta^*$ and
    $\nabla_\vartheta\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\Theta^*(w)}](\vartheta^*_k(w)) = 0$ for every $k$ (implicit function theorem);
    $S(w) := \partial\Theta^*/\partial w$ ($S_k := \partial\vartheta^*_k/\partial w\in\R^{p\times K}$) is given by
    $S = -[\nabla^2_\Theta\mathcal{G}_w]^{-1}\partial_w\nabla_\Theta\mathcal{G}_w$ at $(\Theta^*(w),w)$. For $m$
    particles with $n_k$ of them in cluster $k$ ($w^m_k := n_k/m$), the configuration $\Theta^*_m(w^m)$ with $n_k$
    copies of $\vartheta^*_k(w^m)$ is a critical point of $J_m$ ($\mu_\Theta = \mu_{w^m}$ there). Its whitened
    Hessian splits (Proposition K(i), applied cluster by cluster) into the separation blocks
    $\sigma_0I - R^G[\mu_{w^m}](\vartheta^*_k(w^m))$, each with multiplicity $n_k - 1$, and the collective block
    $\operatorname{diag}(w^m)^{-1/2}\cdot G_K^{1/2}\nabla^2_\Theta\mathcal{G}_{w^m}G_K^{1/2}\cdot\operatorname{diag}(w^m)^{-1/2}$
    (the $K$-atom Hessian, whitened and weighted). Hence $\Theta^*_m(w^m)$ is a strict local minimiser of $J_m$
    exactly when $w^m$ lies in the \emph{admissible set}
    \[
      W_{\mathrm{adm}} := \bigl\{w : \sigma_0\Ginv - R[\mu_{\Theta^*(w)}](\vartheta^*_k(w))\succ0\text{ for every }k
      \text{ and }\nabla^2\mathcal{G}_w(\Theta^*(w))\succ0\bigr\},
    \]
    a neighbourhood of $\bar w$ (both conditions are open and hold at $\bar w$ by (H) and the assumption), and a
    saddle when the splitting condition fails at some cluster: a cluster can then split --- by Proposition K's local separation inside its basin (sub-clusters along
    the unstable direction, the cluster weights unchanged) or by particles migrating to other basins; the numerics
    show both (this section, referee rounds 9 and 10).
  \item \emph{(the stationary distance)} Under the shared-particle coupling of Theorem D$'''$ with i.i.d.\ cluster
    labels of law $\bar w$ (the $m$ particles are the first $m$ of the $M$ reference particles), suppose
    $w^m, w^M\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and that the $m$-system converges to $\Theta^*_m(w^m)$ and the reference to
    $\Theta^*_M(w^M)$. Then the coupling distance converges to
    \begin{align*}
      D_\infty &= \sum_kw^m_k\norm{\vartheta^*_k(w^m) - \vartheta^*_k(w^M)}^2_{\Ginv}\\
      &= \sum_kw^m_k\norm{S_k(w^M)(w^m - w^M)}^2_{\Ginv} + O(\norm{w^m - w^M}^3),
    \end{align*}
    and, with $\tilde S_k := G^{-1/2}S_k(\bar w)$ (replacing $S(w^M)$ by $S(\bar w)$ costs a relative
    $O(\norm{w^M - \bar w})$) and $\E[(w^m - w^M)(w^m - w^M)^\T] = (1/m - 1/M)\tilde C$,
    $\tilde C := \operatorname{diag}(\bar w) - \bar w\bar w^\T$ (multinomial counts of the shared labels),
    conditionally on the admissibility of both weights,
    \[
      \E[D_\infty\mid w^m, w^M\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}] = (1/m - 1/M)\cdot\sum_k\bar w_k\tr\bigl(\tilde S_k\tilde C\tilde S_k^\T\bigr)
      + O(m^{-3/2}) + O(m^{-1}M^{-1/2}):
    \]
    the $1/m$ law with an explicit constant, and the $(1/m - 1/M)$ law of the shared-particle coupling observed in
    Theorem D$'$'s numerics.
  \item \emph{(the fluctuations)} As $M\to\infty$ ($w^M\to\bar w$) and then $m\to\infty$,
    $\sqrt m(w^m - \bar w)\to\mathcal{N}(0,\tilde C)$ and $\sqrt m\cdot(\vartheta_i - \bar\vartheta_i)\to S_{k(i)}\xi$
    with $\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tilde C)$ for every particle $i$ of cluster $k(i)$: the fluctuations of the coupling
    are Gaussian, carried entirely by the cluster weights, and all particles of a cluster are displaced by the same
    vector.
  \item \emph{(admissibility)} Let
    $\delta_w := \sup\{\delta : \norm{w - \bar w}_\infty\le\delta\Rightarrow w\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}\} > 0$. By
    Hoeffding's inequality for each multinomial coordinate,
    $\mathbb{P}(w^m\notin W_{\mathrm{adm}})\le2K\exp(-2m\delta_w^2)$: the finite-$m$ configuration is a strict local
    minimiser with probability exponentially close to one, the exceptional event being the one on which the realised weights make the replicated configuration a saddle: a
    cluster of the $m$-system then splits, locally or by migration to other basins --- in the latter case with
    Remark D$'''$.2's floor on a warm start.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}
\status{Formal: (i) and the algebra of (ii)--(iv) are exact (the block splitting is Proposition K(i) cluster by
cluster, confirmed on $J_{10}$ to $8\cdot10^{-12}$ in referee round 9; implicit function theorem; the multinomial
covariance of shared labels; Hoeffding), the convergence of both systems to the stated configurations is assumed (it
is what Theorem D$'''$ proves for $m\ge m_0$ and what the numerics show for $m = 16$--$256$ at admissible weights; at
inadmissible weights the $m$-system sits on the saddle for a long time and then leaves it, numerics below). Checked:
\code{weighted_stationary_atoms}, \code{weight_sensitivity}, \code{predicted_stationary_D} in
\code{chaos_learning.py}; on the instance below the replicated weighted stationary point is a critical point of
$J_{16}$ to $10^{-10}$, the linearisation $\vartheta^*(\bar w + \mathrm{d}w) - \vartheta^* = S\cdot\mathrm{d}w$ has a
second-order remainder, and the per-cluster displacements of the coupled run point along $S_k(w^m - w^M)$ (test and
table below). The proposition is the sharp form of Theorem D$'''$'s constant:
$S = -H_w^{-1}\partial_w\nabla\mathcal{G}_w$ has the structure (stiffness)$^{-1}$ $\times$ (feature correlations), of
which $C_1 = 4e^{2\Lambda_1}(L_\vartheta^G)^2K_\Phi^2/\kappa^2$ is the worst-case envelope ($\kappa^{-1}$ for the
stiffness, $L_\vartheta^GK_\Phi$ for the correlations).}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.6}[the regime of the theorem, and what the numerics test]
The sample size $m_0 = 16K_\Phi^2/\theta^2$ from which the theorem's tail is in force is astronomically large on
realistic instances ($10^{23}$ below): $\theta$ carries $\varrho'\kappa^2e^{-2\Lambda_1}/(L_\vartheta^G)^3$ from the
per-particle bootstrap of Step~6, because the interaction's effect on one particle is bounded by
$(L_\vartheta^G)^2D^{1/2}$ and divided by the stiffness $\kappa$. This is not an artefact of the proof's constants
only: Proposition D$'''$.5 says the particles of a cluster are displaced by $S_k(w^m - w^M)$, of order
$\norm{S}m^{-1/2}$ with $\norm{S}\sim(\text{stiffness})^{-1}$, which exceeds any fixed $\varrho'$ for $m$ below
$(\norm{S}/\varrho')^2$ --- on the instance below the $m = 16$ particles sit $0.4$--$1.4$ whitened units from their
reference copies, outside the neighbourhood $N_{\varrho'}$ in which $\mathcal{V}[\mu_{\bar\Theta}]$ is convex, at the replicated stationary configuration $\Theta^*_{16}(w^m)$ of
their own $J_{16}$ --- a strict local minimiser when $w^m\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and a saddle otherwise (Proposition
D$'''$.5(i), (iv); one of the four $m = 16$ seeds below). Hypothesis (H) is nevertheless the right object: it is the condition
under which the reference configuration is stable to splitting, and the $m$-system's configuration is the same
configuration with perturbed weights. What the numerics test is therefore (a) hypothesis (H) at the stationary
measure and along the flow, (b) the mechanism --- the coupled distance saturates (no growth) and follows the $1/m$
law --- and (c) the sharp constant of Proposition D$'''$.5; the theorem's own constant $C_1$ is reported for the
record.
\end{rmk}

\begin{thm}{Corollary}{D$'''$.7}[the real loop on a warm start: uniform in time through Theorem J]
Let $\mu^* = \sum_k\bar w_k\delta_{\vartheta^*_k}$ be a $K$-atom stationary measure as in Proposition D$'''$.5,
satisfying the splitting condition $\sigma_0\Ginv - R[\mu^*](\vartheta^*_k)\succ0$ at every atom and the
non-degeneracy of the $K$-atom Hessian, and let $w\mapsto\Theta^*(w)$ be the weighted stationary point of
D$'''$.5(i). Fix $m\le M$ and cluster weights $w^m, w^M\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$ (Proposition D$'''$.5(i)), and let
$\Theta^*_m :=$ the configuration with $n_k = mw^m_k$ copies of $\vartheta^*_k(w^m)$ (likewise $\Theta^*_M$). Both are
non-degenerate local minimisers of $J_m$ and $J_M$ (Proposition D$'''$.5(i)), hence hyperbolic critical points, and
Theorem J applies to each loop: there are $k_0$, $\rho_0$, $C$ (depending on $\Theta^*_m$ and $\Theta^*_M$, hence on
$m$, $M$ and the weights) such that for $k_e\ge k_0$ the real closed loop \textup{(CL)} with $m$ kernels started at
$\norm{\Theta(0) - \Theta^*_m}_{\Ginv}\le\rho_0$, $\norm{e(0)}\le R$ converges to its $T_0$-periodic orbit
$\Theta_p^m$, and likewise the $M$-kernel loop to $\Theta_p^M$. Under the shared-particle coupling (the $m$ kernels
are the first $m$ of the $M$, the plants are driven by the same reference) the coupled distance
$D(t) := \frac1m\sum_i\norm{\vartheta_i(t) - \bar\vartheta_i(t)}^2_{\Ginv}$ satisfies, for all $t\ge0$,
\begin{multline*}
  D(t)^{1/2}\le D_{\mathrm{orb}}^{1/2} + Ce^{-\mu t}\bigl(\norm{\Theta(0) - \Theta_p^m(0)}_{\Ginv} + \norm{x(0) - x_p^m(0)}\bigr)\\
  + Ce^{-\mu t}\bigl(\norm{\bar\Theta(0) - \Theta_p^M(0)}_{\Ginv} + \norm{\bar x(0) - x_p^M(0)}\bigr),
\end{multline*}
with $\mu = (\lambda_*/k_e)(1 - \kappa_J)$ the rate of Theorem J(c) ($\kappa_J$ its small parameter; the smaller of
the two loops' rates) and
\begin{align*}
  D_{\mathrm{orb}} &:= \sup_t\frac1m\sum_i\norm{\Theta_p^m(t)_i - \Theta_p^M(t)_i}^2_{\Ginv}\\
  &= \sum_kw^m_k\norm{\vartheta^*_k(w^m) - \vartheta^*_k(w^M)}^2_{\Ginv} + O(1/k_e)\\
  &= \sum_kw^m_k\norm{S_k(w^m - w^M)}^2_{\Ginv} + O(\norm{w^m - w^M}^3) + O(1/k_e):
\end{align*}
the coupled distance of the real loop is bounded uniformly in time, converges to the distance between the two
periodic orbits, and that distance is Proposition D$'''$.5's stationary distance up to the $O(1/k_e)$ offsets of
Theorem J(a),(d) (which differ between the two loops by $O(k_e^{-1}\norm{w^m - w^M}) + O(k_e^{-2})$, the first-order
offset of a replicated configuration being a smooth function of the $K$-atom data $(\Theta^*(w),w)$ by permutation
equivariance). In particular, for cluster labels i.i.d.\ of law $\bar w$ and conditionally on both weights being
admissible and both starts lying in their basins,
$\E D_{\mathrm{orb}} = (1/m - 1/M)\sum_k\bar w_k\tr(\tilde S_k\tilde C\tilde S_k^\T) + O(m^{-3/2}) + O(1/k_e)$.
\end{thm}
\status{Proved as stated (Theorem J(a),(c),(d) applied to each loop, the triangle inequality in the $\Ginv$ metric,
and Proposition D$'''$.5(ii) for the distance between the critical points), under Theorem J's hypotheses for both

\begin{proof}
Theorem J(c) for the $m$-kernel loop gives
$\norm{\Theta(t) - \Theta_p^m(t)}_{\Ginv}\le Ce^{-\mu t}(\norm{\Theta(0) - \Theta_p^m(0)}_{\Ginv} + \norm{x(0) - x_p^m(0)})$
and the same for the $M$-kernel loop; the first $m$ rows of the $M$-loop are the reference particles
$\bar\vartheta_i$. For the $(m\times p)$-row norm
$\frac1m\sum_i\norm{\cdot}^2_{\Ginv}\le\norm{\cdot}^2_{\Ginv}/m\le\norm{\cdot}^2_{\Ginv}$ (the full configuration
norm dominates the mean square over the first $m$ rows), so by the triangle inequality
$D(t)^{1/2}\le[\frac1m\sum_i\norm{\Theta_p^m(t)_i - \Theta_p^M(t)_i}^2]^{1/2} + (\text{the two transients})$.
Theorem J(a) places each orbit within $C/k_e$ of its critical point, uniformly in $t$, so the first term is
$\sum_kw^m_k\norm{\vartheta^*_k(w^m) - \vartheta^*_k(w^M)}^2_{\Ginv}$ up to $O(1/k_e)$ (the two offsets are
$O(1/k_e)$ each, with $O(k_e^{-2})$ remainders; at a replicated configuration the first-order offset is the same
vector for all particles of a cluster and depends smoothly on the $K$-atom data $(\Theta^*(w),w)$, by the permutation
equivariance of \textup{(CL)} and the block splitting of Proposition D$'''$.5(i), so the two loops' offsets differ by
$O(k_e^{-1}\norm{w^m - w^M}) + O(k_e^{-2})$ --- the two loops live on $\R^{mp}$ and $\R^{Mp}$, and it is this
reduction to the $K$-atom data that makes them comparable), and Proposition D$'''$.5(ii) expands the distance between
the critical points in $w^m - w^M$. The expectation statement is D$'''$.5(ii)'s.
\end{proof}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.8}[what the corollary does and does not say]
It transfers the uniform-in-time statement to the real loop in the regime where Theorem J applies to both loops ---
a warm start in the basins of two hyperbolic minimisers at a gain $k_e$ above both thresholds. Theorem J's constants
at a replicated configuration are controlled by the $K$-atom data (the Hessian of $J_m$ there is the collective block
$\nabla^2\mathcal{G}_{w^m}\oplus{}$the separation blocks $\sigma_0\Ginv - R_k$ of Proposition K(i), both independent
of $m$ beyond the weights), so the threshold and the basin are expected to be uniform in $m$ for configurations with
the same weights; the corollary does not prove this, and the cold start --- where the coupled distance of the real
loop grew in Theorem D$''$'s experiment --- is outside it on the same two counts as in Remark D$'''$.2.
\end{rmk}

\begin{thm}{Proposition}{D$'''$.9}[the leak toward a measure-valued prior, and the design of the window]
Let $\nu = \sum_{j=1}^{K_0}q_j\delta_{\vartheta^0_j}$ be a prior measure with distinct atoms and let the leak be the
$G$-gradient of the distance-to-support potential
\[
  V_\nu(\vartheta) := \tfrac12\min_j\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0_j}^2_{\Ginv},\qquad
  -G\nabla V_\nu(\vartheta) = -(\vartheta - \vartheta^0_{j(\vartheta)}),\qquad
  j(\vartheta) := \operatorname*{argmin}_j\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0_j}_{\Ginv}
\]
(the \emph{nearest-atom leak}: inside each Voronoi cell of $\nu$ it is the $\sigma$-modification toward that cell's
atom; the barycentric OT leak of Theorem C$'$ is its mass-constrained relative). Write
\textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^\nu$)} and \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0^\nu$)} for \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m$)} and
\textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0$)} with $\sigma_0(\vartheta - \vartheta^0)$ replaced by
$\sigma_0(\vartheta - \vartheta^0_{j(\vartheta)})$, and $\mathcal{V}^\nu[\mu] := V_L[\mu] + \sigma_0V_\nu$ for the
first-variation potential.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \emph{(the theorems carry over)} Assume, in addition to the hypotheses of Theorem D$'''$ for
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0^\nu$)}, that the assignment is locally constant along the flow: for every $s\ge0$ the
    neighbourhood $N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_s)$ meets no Voronoi boundary of $\nu$ (in particular when the gap between
    the distances to the two nearest atoms exceeds $2\varrho'$ on $\supp\mu_s$, the $\Ginv$-distance to the boundary
    being at least half the gap). Then Theorem D$'''$, Proposition D$'''$.5 and Corollary D$'''$.7 hold for
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^\nu$)} with the same constants, $R_\Theta$ replaced by
    $\max\{R_0, L_\vartheta^G(F_\rho + K_\Phi)/\sigma_0\} + \max_j\norm{\vartheta^0_j - \vartheta^0_1}_{\Ginv}$ and
    $\vartheta^0$ by $\vartheta^0_{j(\cdot)}$ in the a priori bounds, and Theorem J's basin radius $\rho_0$ at the
    replicated configuration taken below $\min_k\operatorname{dist}_{\Ginv}(\vartheta^*_k,\partial\,\mathrm{cell}_k)$.
    The masses $q_j$ enter the nearest-atom leak only through $\supp\nu$; they matter in (ii), and they are what the
    mass constraint of the OT leak (Theorem C$'$, Remark D$'''$.10) acts on.
  \item \emph{(the stationary residual and the window)} If $\nu$ is placed on an exact representation of the plant
    on the orbit --- $K_0 = K$ atoms $\vartheta^0_k$ with $\sum_kq_k\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta^0_k) = f$ on
    $\supp\rho$ --- then $\mu^* := \sum_kq_k\delta_{\vartheta^0_k}$ is a stationary measure of
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0^\nu$)} with $r_{\mu^*} = 0$, $R[\mu^*]\equiv0$, and the window of Remark D$'''$.1 has
    its maximal margin: $\kappa_R[\mu^*](A) = 0$ for every set $A$ ($R[\mu^*]\equiv0$) and $\kappa = \sigma_0$, the
    only restriction on $\varrho'$ being (i)'s Voronoi hypothesis, against
    $\kappa = \sigma_0 - \kappa_R[\mu^*_\sigma](N_{\varrho'})$ for the $\sigma$-modification's stationary measure
    $\mu^*_\sigma$, whose residual is the leak's bias. For a prior $\nu_\delta$ displaced from the exact
    representation the residual of the stationary measure is $O(\delta)$ and
    $\kappa_R[\mu^*_{\nu_\delta}] = O(\delta)$: the margin decreases with the misplacement, and the window closes
    when the displacement makes the residual-weighted curvature at an atom reach $\sigma_0$.
  \item \emph{(the floors)} Under the shared-particle coupling the stationary distances are the weight-sensitivity
    laws of Proposition D$'''$.5 at parameter level,
    $\E[D_\infty\mid w^m, w^M\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}] = (1/m - 1/M)\sum_k\bar w_k\tr(\tilde S_k\tilde C\tilde S_k^\T)
    + O(m^{-3/2}) + O(m^{-1}M^{-1/2})$, and at function level,
    \begin{gather*}
      \E\norm{\fhat_{\Theta^*(w^m)} - \fhat_{\Theta^*(w^M)}}^2_{L^2(\rho)}
      = (1/m - 1/M)\cdot\tr(F\tilde CF^\T)/N + O(m^{-3/2}),\\
      F_{\cdot j} := \partial\fhat_w/\partial w_j
      = \tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta^*_j) + \sum_kw_k\nabla_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta^*_k)S_{k\cdot j},
    \end{gather*}
    with $S$ the sensitivity of the weighted stationary point of \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0^\nu$)} to the weights.
    Neither floor vanishes for the matching prior: away from $\bar w$ the weighted stationary point does not stay on
    the zero-residual manifold (the leak pulls the amplitudes back to the prior while the fit asks for
    $a_k\propto1/w_k$); on the instance both sensitivities rise with the amplitudes of the stationary atoms along the
    amplitude-scaled priors --- a numerical trend, not a law (at a fixed amplitude the width changes the
    sensitivity, Table~\ref{tab:thmDppp:design}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}
\status{(i) proved (the proofs of Theorem D$'''$, Proposition D$'''$.5 and Corollary D$'''$.7 use the leak only

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.10}[design]
The leak's prior is a design input of the window. The $\sigma$-modification's single $\vartheta^0$ biases every
kernel toward one point and leaves a residual at the fitted configuration --- the residual-weighted curvature that
bounds the window from below and slows the relaxation ($\kappa = 0.00196$ on the instance of this section, the
theorem's relaxation time $1/\kappa = 511$ slow units). A prior measure whose atoms sit on the plant's structure
removes the bias and gives the maximal margin $\kappa = \sigma_0$ ($1/\kappa = 141$ slow units, 3.6 times shorter;
the observed e-folding times of the coupled distance are 3.3 times shorter in the median, numerics below), at the
price of a larger floor at both levels (the stationary atoms have the full amplitudes, to which the weight
sensitivity is proportional): the classical trade-off between bias and variance, here between the window's margin
and the coupling's floor. A misplaced prior does worse than no structured prior (centres off by $0.6$, amplitudes
$\times1.2$ or widths $\times0.7$ close the window, which the $\sigma$-modification keeps open with margin
$0.0020$), and a shrunk prior does better than the matching one (amplitudes $\times0.5$--$0.7$: margin above
$\sigma_0$ at the uniform weights, both floors lower, all four tested unbalanced weights admissible --- with
splitting margins above $\sigma_0$ at one of them and slightly below at the other three; numerics below and
Remark D$'''$.11). The barycentric OT leak of Theorem C$'$ adds a mass constraint to the same geometry --- each
prior atom must receive its mass --- and so acts on the cluster weights themselves, the source of the floor; its
interplay with the weight fluctuation (an equal-mass Lloyd dynamics against the fit) is the natural next step
(Section~\ref{sec:program}, branch 8).
\end{rmk}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Reading.} The prior is a design input on three counts. Its placement sets the stationary residual,
hence the window's lower end and the relaxation rate (the matching prior: margin $\sigma_0$, the theorem's
relaxation time 3.6 times shorter, the observed one 3.3 times); the amplitudes it induces set the floors (which rise
with them along the amplitude-scaled priors, so removing the bias raises the floors by 1.6--2.2 times); and the sign
of the residual it leaves at each cluster sets the admissible set of weights (overshoot splits, undershoot
stabilises). A shrunk prior --- amplitudes 0.5--0.7 of the plant's --- wins on all three at a fit error of
0.06--0.09; a misplaced one (centres off by more than a bump width, amplitudes too large, widths too narrow) closes
the window.

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.11}[undershoot stabilises, overshoot splits]
For a Gaussian kernel, $\partial^2_{cc}\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta) = -(a/s^2)(1 - (x - c)^2/s^2)\varphi$ is negative at
the centre, so the $(c,c)$ entry of $R[\mu](\vartheta_k) = \langle r_\mu\nabla^2\tilde\Phi\rangle_\rho$ has the sign
of $-a_k\cdot r_\mu$ near the centre: a cluster whose kernel overshoots the plant there ($r$ of the sign opposite to
$a_k$) gets $R\succ0$ in the centre direction --- the separation block $\sigma_0\Ginv - R$ loses margin, and the
cluster splits once $\kappa_R$ exceeds $\sigma_0$ --- while an undershooting cluster gets $R\prec0$ and a splitting
margin above $\sigma_0$. For the matching prior this is exactly what Table~\ref{tab:thmDppp:adm} shows: at every
tested weight the over-weighted atom overshoots at its centre ($\fhat/f = 1.07$--$1.10$), its $R_{cc}$ is positive
and the top eigenvector of $R^G$ is the centre direction, $(0.01, 1.00, -0.02)$ in $(a,c,s)$, while the
under-weighted atoms have $R_{cc} < 0$; the shrunk prior undershoots at the uniform weights and at $(7,5,4)/16$, and
at the three more unbalanced weights its over-weighted atom overshoots slightly ($\fhat/f = 1.007$--$1.028$), taking
the margin just below $\sigma_0$. The two other instabilities of this section and Section~\ref{sec:thmK} have a
different anatomy (referee round 10): the $\sigma$-modification's inadmissible seed (weight $9/16$ on the first
bump) still undershoots at its centre ($R_{cc} < 0$), its unstable direction being a mixed amplitude--width
direction --- the broad kernel (width $0.88$ against the bump's $0.5$) overshoots in its tails, through the
cross-curvature $\langle r\varphi(x - c)^2/s^3\rangle_\rho$; and Proposition K's collapsed kernel owes its
$R_{cc} = +0.055$ to the overshoot over the negative bump and to the undershoot of the outer bumps beyond its
inflection points, where $1 - (x - c)^2/s^2 < 0$, not to the region between the bumps. It is an observation about
the Gaussian kernel's second derivative, not a theorem: the width and amplitude directions carry other signs, and
the residual's profile matters; the splitting margin itself is the quantity to compute.
\end{rmk}

\begin{thm}{Proposition}{D$'''$.12}[the mass constraint removes the weight-fluctuation floor]
Let $\nu = \sum_{j=1}^{K_0}q_j\delta_{\vartheta^0_j}$ be a prior measure with distinct atoms and masses $q_j > 0$,
$\Delta_\nu := \min_{j\ne k}\norm{\vartheta^0_j - \vartheta^0_k}_{\Ginv}$, and let the leak be the barycentric OT leak
of Theorem C$'$ toward $\nu$ for the cost $\norm{\cdot}^2_{\Ginv}$: with $\gamma = \gamma(\Theta)$ a vertex optimal
plan from $\mu_\Theta$ to $\nu$ (row sums $1/m$, column sums $q_j$) and $\bar T_i := m\sum_j\gamma_{ij}\vartheta^0_j$
its barycentric projection,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd\vartheta_i}{\dd s} = G\langle\nabla_\vartheta(r_\Theta\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_i))\rangle_\rho
  - \sigma_0(\vartheta_i - \bar T_i),
  \tag{$\mathfrak{A}_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$}
\end{equation}
the $G$-gradient flow of $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}(\Theta) := m\bar L(\mu_\Theta) + (\sigma_0m/2)W^2_{\Ginv}(\mu_\Theta,\nu)$
wherever the optimal plan is unique (Danskin's theorem, as in Theorem C$'$(i)), with the vertex-selection solutions
of Section~\ref{sec:thmCprime} where it is not. Its mean-field form is the \emph{Laguerre-cell leak}: for $\mu$
giving no mass to their boundaries, the optimal plan from $\mu$ to the atomic $\nu$ is induced by the map
$T_\mu(\vartheta) = \vartheta^0_j$ on the Laguerre cell
\[
  L_j[\mu] := \bigl\{\vartheta : \norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0_j}^2_{\Ginv} - \psi_j
  \le\norm{\vartheta - \vartheta^0_k}^2_{\Ginv} - \psi_k\text{ for all }k\bigr\},
\]
the dual weights $\psi$ fixed by $\mu(L_j[\mu]) = q_j$ (semi-discrete optimal transport~\cite{S42}; for a
disconnected support the boundaries in the gaps are not unique, but $T_\mu$ is unique on $\supp\mu$), and
\textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0^{\mathrm{OT}}$)} is \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_0$)} with the leak
$-\sigma_0(\vartheta - T_{\mu_s}(\vartheta))$: the nearest-atom leak of Proposition D$'''$.9 with the Voronoi cells
replaced by cells of prescribed masses. Call $m$ \emph{commensurate} with $\nu$ if $mq_j\in\N$ for every $j$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \emph{(conservation against constraint)} Along \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^\nu$)} on the good event
    $\mathcal{G}_S$ of Theorem D$'''$ (Proposition D$'''$.9(i)) no particle changes cell, so the cluster masses
    $w_k(\Theta_s) := (1/m)\#\{i : j(\vartheta_i(s)) = k\}$ are conserved: $w^m(s) = w^m(0)$, the multinomial
    sample of the initial cloud, and the stationary floor of Proposition D$'''$.5 is the memory of the initial
    condition that this conservation law carries. Along \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$)} the masses
    assigned to the atoms are constrained instead: $\sum_i\gamma_{ij}(s) = q_j$ for every $s$.
  \item \emph{(the frozen plan)} If $m$ is commensurate, every vertex of the transportation polytope is integral
    --- $\gamma_{ij}\in\{0,1/m\}$: each particle is assigned to one atom $\pi(i)$ and atom $j$ receives exactly
    $mq_j$ particles --- so $\bar T_i = \vartheta^0_{\pi(i)}$ and \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$)} is
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^\nu$)} with the Voronoi assignment $j(\cdot)$ replaced by the plan's assignment $\pi$,
    locally constant in $\Theta$ wherever the optimal plan is unique; there $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$ is $C^2$ with the
    leak's Hessian $\sigma_0\Ginv\otimes I$. Theorem D$'''$'s synchronous coupling applies to
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$)} only under the hypothesis of Proposition D$'''$.9(i) read with the
    plan --- for every $s\ge0$ the plan assigns each particle to the atom of its reference's Laguerre cell
    $L_\cdot[\mu_s]$, and $N_{\varrho'}(\supp\mu_s)$ meets no Laguerre boundary --- and at $s = 0$ this requires the
    sample to be \emph{balanced}, $w^m(0) = q$ (the mean-field flow of the mixture start has masses $q$ from the
    start): an event of probability of order $m^{-(K_0-1)/2}$ under an i.i.d.\ start --- $0.053$, $0.014$,
    $0.0034$, $0.0016$ at $m = 15$, $60$, $240$, $510$ on the instance, $\approx0.83/m$. On that event Theorem
    D$'''$, Proposition D$'''$.5 and Corollary D$'''$.7 go through with the same constants except for Step~8, whose
    McDiarmid bound for the reference's sampling term is written for i.i.d.\ particles and would have to be redone
    for the stratified sample ($mq_k$ particles i.i.d.\ within each class). For a generic sample the plan reassigns
    at least $\frac12\norm{w^m(0) - q}_1$ of the mass at $s = 0$ and the shared-particle coupling carries that
    mismatch for ever ((v)); what is proved about the OT dynamics in general is the local equivalence above and the
    stationary statement (iii).
  \item \emph{(the stationary measure does not depend on $m$)} Let
    $\Theta^*(q) = (\vartheta^*_1,\dots,\vartheta^*_{K_0})$ be the weighted stationary point of $\mathcal{G}_q$ with
    the prior atoms (Proposition D$'''$.5 with $w = q$, each class $k$ leaking toward $\vartheta^0_k$ as in
    Proposition D$'''$.9) and $\varepsilon := \max_k\norm{\vartheta^*_k - \vartheta^0_k}_{\Ginv}$. If
    $\varepsilon < \Delta_\nu/2$ then, for every commensurate $m$, the replicated configuration $\Theta^*_m(q)$
    ($mq_k$ particles at $\vartheta^*_k$) has the diagonal plan (class $k$ $\to$ atom $k$) as its unique optimal
    plan, is a critical point of $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$, and is a strict local minimiser iff $q\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$
    (its Hessian is Proposition D$'''$.5's, with separation blocks $\sigma_0I - R^G_k$ of multiplicities
    $mq_k - 1$). The stationary measure $\mu^*(q) := \sum_kq_k\delta_{\vartheta^*_k}$ and the learned function
    $\fhat^*(q) = \sum_kq_k\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta^*_k)$ are therefore the same for every commensurate $m$, and
    the stationary floors vanish:
    \[
      W_{\Ginv}(\mu^*_m,\mu^*_M) = 0,\qquad\norm{\fhat^*_m - \fhat^*_M}_{L^2(\rho)} = 0\qquad
      \text{for all commensurate }m, M,
    \]
    against $\E[D_\infty\mid\mathrm{adm}] = (1/m - 1/M)\sum_k\bar w_k\tr(\tilde S_k\tilde C\tilde S_k^\T)$ and
    $(1/m - 1/M)\tr(F\tilde CF^\T)/N$ under the nearest-atom leak (Propositions D$'''$.5 and D$'''$.9(iii)), whose
    stationary measures are moreover at squared $W_2$-distance
    $\frac12\norm{w^m - w^M}_1\cdot O(\Delta_\nu^2) = O(\Delta_\nu^2m^{-1/2})$ from each other ($W_2$ itself
    $O(\Delta_\nu m^{-1/4})$) --- the cross-cluster transport of the weight mismatch, which the shared-particle
    distance $D$ does not see. The price is the bias of the masses: the stationary residual
    $r^*(q) = f - \sum_kq_k\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta^*_k)$ vanishes iff $\nu$ carries an exact representation of
    the plant \emph{with its masses}, $\sum_kq_k\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta^0_k) = f$ on $\supp\rho$; for masses
    $q_\delta$ displaced from those of the exact representation the residual is $O(\delta)$ and the window's margin
    decreases with $\delta$ (Proposition D$'''$.9(ii) with the masses as the parameter), and the designed masses
    must themselves be admissible, $q\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$: past the admissible set the over-weighted equal-mass
    cluster splits (Remark D$'''$.11).
  \item \emph{(incommensurate $m$: the quantisation remainder)} For any $m$ and any configuration a vertex optimal
    plan splits at most $K_0 - 1$ particles between two or more atoms (a vertex of the transportation polytope is
    supported on a forest of the bipartite graph with $m + K_0$ nodes, so on at most $m + K_0 - 1$ edges, every
    particle carrying at least one). At a stationary configuration whose unsplit particles are collapsed by class
    (the minimisers of Proposition D$'''$.5's type) a split particle sits where the fit force balances the pull
    toward its barycentric target $\bar T_i\in\operatorname{conv}\{\vartheta^0_j : \gamma_{ij} > 0\}$ --- a
    \emph{bridge particle} between the clusters --- so the stationary measure differs from $\mu^*(q)$ by at most
    $(K_0 - 1)/m$ of mass at the bridge positions and by the $O(K_0/m)$ shift of the class weights:
    \begin{gather*}
      W^2_{\Ginv}(\mu^*_m,\mu^*(q))\le4(K_0 - 1)R_\Theta^2/m + O(K_0^2\norm{S}^2/m^2),\\
      \norm{\fhat^*_m - \fhat^*(q)}_{L^2(\rho)}\le2(K_0 - 1)K_\Phi/m + O(K_0(K_\Phi + L_\vartheta^G\norm{S})/m),
    \end{gather*}
    $R_\Theta$ the a priori radius of Step~0 (with the hull of $\supp\nu$). The orders are the content, not the
    constants --- on the instance $R_\Theta = 2865$ makes the first bound $4\cdot10^6$ against a measured 1.0--1.4
    at $m = 16$, and the second $1.00$ against $0.018$: the function-level floor is $O(1/m^2)$ in square, one order
    in $m$ below the $(1/m - 1/M)$ law, and the $W^2$ floor is $O(1/m)$ against the nearest-atom leak's
    $O(m^{-1/2})$ of (iii) --- half an order gained in $W^2$, a full order at function level --- with deterministic
    constants set by the plan's split pattern (measured $m\cdot W^2(\mu^*_m,\nu)\approx20 = 0.5\Delta_\nu^2$ and
    $m\cdot\norm{\fhat^*_m - f}/\norm{f}\approx0.55$).
  \item \emph{(the transit)} From a cloud with cluster masses $w^m\ne q$ the plan assigns at least
    $\frac12\norm{w^m - q}_1$ of the mass (up to the split particles) of the over-weighted clusters to under-weighted
    atoms, and these particles migrate between the clusters against the fit force at the leak's rate --- for the
    pure leak a migrating particle crosses the Voronoi midpoint at $s = \ln2/\sigma_0$. During the transit the
    synchronous coupling of Theorem D$'''$ sees the mass migrating in one system and not in the other at a distance
    of order $\Delta_\nu$, $D = O(\Delta_\nu^2\norm{w^m - w^M}_1)$ --- it is not a coupling the OT leak respects ---
    $W^2_{\Ginv}(\mu_m,\mu_M)$ is of the same order $O(\Delta_\nu^2m^{-1/2})$, the sampling term of the cluster
    masses, and the function-level distance is $O(K_\Phi\norm{w^m - w^M}_1) = O(m^{-1/2})$. After the transit the
    two measure-level distances vanish on the commensurate sizes ((iii)), while the synchronous $D$ converges from
    below to the plateau $\sum_{k\ne k'}p_{kk'}\Delta^2_{kk'}$ of the mass reassigned in one system and not in the
    other (0.95--0.96 of it at $s = 600$ on the instance): the memory of the initial masses, erased from the
    stationary measure, is kept by the shared-particle coupling. A mean-field flow whose clusters start with masses
    $w\ne q$ has the same transit --- the Laguerre boundaries cut the over-weighted clusters and the mass
    $\frac12\norm{w - q}_1$ migrates --- but the mixture start of the numerics has mean-field masses $q$, so there
    the $M$-references transit only as finite samples and are poor stand-ins for the mean-field flow during the
    transit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.13}[conservation, constraint, and what the mean-field limit forgets]
Theorem D$'''$ bounds the coupled distance uniformly in time, and Proposition D$'''$.5 says what the bound's constant
is made of: the $m$-system's memory of its initial cluster masses, which the nearest-atom leak conserves. The OT
leak replaces the conservation law by a constraint and the memory is erased from the stationary measure --- the
finite-$m$ stationary measure \emph{is} the mean-field one on the commensurate sizes, and within $(K_0 - 1)/m$ of
mass of it otherwise --- though not from the shared-particle coupling, whose synchronous distance keeps the plateau
of the reassigned particles ((v)). This is a statement about the design of the leak, not about $m$: the same clouds
under the nearest-atom leak keep their floors. Three consequences. (a) The masses join the atoms as design inputs of
the leak's prior --- both are read from the plant's structure (Proposition D$'''$.9(ii)) --- and the admissible set
$W_{\mathrm{adm}}$ of Proposition D$'''$.5, a probability statement about the realised weights under the
nearest-atom leak, becomes a condition on the design, $q\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$, under the OT leak. (b) The
mean-field form of Theorem C$'$'s leak for an atomic prior is the Laguerre-cell leak; its finite-$m$ fluctuation is
not a random displacement of the cells but the split particles --- at most $K_0 - 1$ --- and the migrating ones
during the transit. (c) A soft mass constraint (an entropic or penalised plan) would interpolate between the two
leaks, with a floor set by the stiffness of the penalty; open (Section~\ref{sec:program}, branch 8), as is the
transfer of a learned measure to a plant whose representation weights are unknown, where $q$ itself would have to
be learned.
\end{rmk}

\noindent\emph{Reading.} The two leaks start from the same clouds and end in different worlds. The nearest-atom
leak keeps the clusters' sampled masses for ever, and its stationary distances are what Propositions D$'''$.5 and
D$'''$.9 say: a function-level floor of order $1/m$, and a $W^2$ floor of order $m^{-1/2}$ set by the cross-cluster
transport of the weight mismatch. The OT leak moves one to 21 particles across in about $\ln2/\sigma_0$ slow units
and then settles on the mean-field stationary measure: on the commensurate sizes every distance but the synchronous
one decays to zero (three to four orders of magnitude below the nearest-atom leak's floors after $4.2/\sigma_0$),
and the fit loses the weight bias; on the incommensurate sizes the floors are the one or two bridge particles ---
$O(1/m^2)$ at function level, $O(1/m)$ in $W^2$ --- whose position between the clusters is the price of a mass
constraint the size cannot meet. The masses are a design input with a bias and an admissibility constraint of their

\begin{thm}{Proposition}{D$'''$.15}[the transverse spectrum of a lifted orbit]
Let $\Gamma = (x_p,\Theta_p)$ be a $T_0$-periodic orbit of \textup{(CL)} --- with any leak whose targets are locally
constant near $\Gamma$ and equal within each class --- lying in the collapsed subspace $\mathcal{C}_m(\pi_0)$ with
classes of sizes $n_k$, and let $\Gamma_K = (x_p,(\vartheta_{p,k})_k)$ be the $K_0$-atom orbit it lifts. For each
class $k$ let
\[
  S_k := \Bigl\{\delta\in\R^{n+mp} : \delta x = 0,\ \delta_i = 0\text{ for }i\notin\pi_0^{-1}(k),\
  \sum_{i\in\pi_0^{-1}(k)}\delta_i = 0\Bigr\}\qquad\text{(dimension $(n_k - 1)p$)},
\]
and let $M_k$ be the monodromy over one period of the \emph{ghost equation} $\dot\delta = A_k(t)\delta$,
$A_k(t) := \partial_\vartheta\bigl[G\nabla_\vartheta\bigl(e_p(t)^\T\tilde\Phi(x_p(t);\vartheta)\bigr)\bigr]
\big|_{\vartheta=\vartheta_{p,k}(t)} - \sigma I$ --- the linearisation of one particle of class $k$ driven by the
orbit's plant state, with weight zero in $\fhat$. Then the monodromy matrix $\mathbb{M}$ of $\Gamma$ leaves every
$S_k$ invariant, with $\mathbb{M}|_{S_k} = M_k\otimes I_{n_k-1}$, and on the complementary invariant subspace --- the
plant coordinates and the class means --- it is the monodromy $\mathbb{M}_K$ of $\Gamma_K$ as an orbit of the
weighted $K_0$-atom loop \textup{(CL$_w$)}. Hence the Floquet spectrum of $\Gamma$ is that of $\Gamma_K$ together
with the spectrum of each $M_k$ with multiplicity $n_k - 1$, independent of $m$; $\Gamma$ is exponentially stable iff
$\Gamma_K$ is and every $M_k$ is contracting; and when $\Gamma_K$ is Theorem J's orbit of \textup{(CL$_w$)} at
$\Theta^*(w)$, the exponents of $M_k$ are $(1/k_e)\lambda_j(\sigma_0I - R^G_k) + O(k_e^{-2})$, $j = 1..p$, with
$R^G_k = G^{1/2}R[\mu_{\Theta^*(w)}](\vartheta^*_k)G^{1/2}$ --- Proposition K's separation condition and the
splitting blocks of Proposition D$'''$.5(i), read along the orbit.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
Write the particle law as $\dot\vartheta_i = F(x,e,\vartheta_i) - \sigma(\vartheta_i - T_i)$,
$F(x,e,\vartheta) := G\nabla_\vartheta(e^\T\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta))$, and note that the plant's equation depends on
$\Theta$ only through $\fhat_\Theta(x) = (1/m)\sum_i\tilde\Phi(x;\vartheta_i)$. Along $\Gamma$ the linearised
equations are
$\delta\dot x = (\partial_xf - k_eI - \partial_x\fhat)\delta x
- (1/m)\sum_i\partial_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x_p;\vartheta_i)\delta_i$ and
$\dot\delta_i = (\partial_xF + \partial_eF)\delta x + (\partial_\vartheta F - \sigma I)|_{\vartheta_i}\delta_i$
($e = x - x_m$ gives $\delta e = \delta x$). For $\delta\in S_k$, $\delta x = 0$ and all $\vartheta_i$ with
$i\in\pi_0^{-1}(k)$ equal $\vartheta_{p,k}(t)$, so
$\sum_i\partial_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x_p;\vartheta_i)\delta_i
= \partial_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x_p;\vartheta_{p,k})\sum_{i\in\pi_0^{-1}(k)}\delta_i = 0$: the plant is not
perturbed to first order, each $\delta_i$ obeys $\dot\delta_i = A_k(t)\delta_i$ with the same $A_k$, and
$\sum_{i\in\pi_0^{-1}(k)}\dot\delta_i = A_k\sum\delta_i = 0$. So $S_k$ is invariant and the linearised flow acts on
it as the ghost equation on every component, whence $\mathbb{M}|_{S_k} = M_k\otimes I_{n_k-1}$. The class-constant
perturbations $\{\delta_i = u_{\pi_0(i)}\}$ together with $\delta x$ form the complementary subspace; it is invariant
because particles of one class have identical right-hand sides when their perturbations coincide, and there the
variation of $\fhat$ is $\sum_k(n_k/m)\partial_\vartheta\tilde\Phi(x_p;\vartheta_{p,k})u_k
= \sum_kw_k\partial_\vartheta\tilde\Phi u_k$ --- the linearisation of \textup{(CL$_w$)} along $\Gamma_K$. The
spectral statements follow. For the last one, along Theorem J's orbit of \textup{(CL$_w$)} the tracking error is
$e_p(t) = r_{\Theta^*(w)}(x_m(t))/k_e + O(k_e^{-2})$ and $\vartheta_{p,k}(t) = \vartheta^*_k(w) + O(1/k_e)$ (Theorem
J(a) with the error equation), so the ghost matrix is
$A_k(t) = (1/k_e)\bigl[G\partial^2_\vartheta\bigl(r_{\Theta^*(w)}(x_m(t))\tilde\Phi(x_m(t);\vartheta^*_k)\bigr)
- \sigma_0I\bigr] + O(k_e^{-2})$: a $T_0$-periodic matrix of size $O(1/k_e)$ whose period mean is
$(1/k_e)(GR_k - \sigma_0I) + O(k_e^{-2})$, with $R_k = R[\mu_{\Theta^*(w)}](\vartheta^*_k)$ the residual-weighted
curvature of Theorem D$'''$. First-order averaging of this linear periodic equation gives
$M_k = \exp(T_0(1/k_e)(GR_k - \sigma_0I)) + O(k_e^{-2})$, and $GR_k - \sigma_0I$ is similar to
$G^{1/2}R_kG^{1/2} - \sigma_0I = -(\sigma_0I - R^G_k)$: the exponents are
$(1/k_e)\lambda_j(\sigma_0I - R^G_k) + O(k_e^{-2})$, with no condition on $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.16}[dither and the mass constraint]
With dither (Theorem J$'$: $\tau = \tau_0/k_e$, $\sigma = \sigma_0/k_e$) the leading-order stationary law of the slow
variables is the Gibbs measure $\pi\propto\exp(-J_m/\tau_0)$, and the cluster masses are no longer conserved under
the nearest-atom leak: a particle crosses a Voronoi ridge at the Arrhenius rate $\exp(-\Delta V/\tau_0)$, $\Delta V$
the single-particle barrier out of the configuration --- at least the leak's part
$\sigma_0\Delta_\nu^2/8 = (\sigma_0/2)(\Delta_\nu/2)^2$ at the lowest ridge point, plus the fit's variation --- and
the counts sample the Gibbs law over the count vectors $n$: in the Laplace approximation
$\mathrm{multinomial}(n)\cdot\exp(-m\mathcal{G}^*_{n/m}/\tau_0)$ times the determinants of the separation and
collective blocks of Proposition D$'''$.5(i), for $n/m\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$ (at a saddle weight the split
configuration lies lower, and its soft and unstable separation modes carry entropy that the quadratic approximation
misses). Dither thus does on the Arrhenius time scale, and only in distribution, what the OT leak does
deterministically in $\ln2/\sigma_0$ slow units at $\tau_0 = 0$. Under the OT leak the Gibbs measure is
$\pi^{\mathrm{OT}}\propto\exp(-J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}/\tau_0)$ ($J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$ is locally Lipschitz, piecewise smooth,
with Gaussian tails), the plan's masses are $q$ by construction, and a particle that crosses a Voronoi ridge stays
assigned to its own atom: with integral plans an exchange of the assignments of particles $a\in{}$class $j$ and
$b\in{}$class $k$ lowers the cost only once
$\langle\vartheta_a - \vartheta_b,\vartheta^0_k - \vartheta^0_j\rangle_{\Ginv} > 0$, that is, once they have passed
each other. So the counts by nearest atom leave $q$ at the nearest-atom leak's ridge barrier, but only for a
reversible excursion --- beyond the ridge the leak toward the particle's own atom keeps pulling it back --- of
stationary probability of order $\exp(-\Delta V_{\mathrm{ridge}}/\tau_0)$; exchanges of assignments (whose barrier is
at most the lowest tie point of two particles) leave the masses at $q$ as well. The mass constraint does not raise
the barrier of a crossing; it makes the crossing reversible.
\end{rmk}
\status{Formal (the Gibbs law of a locally Lipschitz potential with Gaussian tails; the Arrhenius estimate; the
Laplace asymptotics).}

\begin{thm}{Proposition}{D$'''$.17}[convergence of the OT dynamics]
Let $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$ be the energy of Proposition D$'''$.12 for the saturated Gaussian features, with $\rho$ the
finite quadrature of the code or the period average of an analytic reference, on the a priori ball of Step~0.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \emph{(a Lyapunov function with a semiconcave part)}
    $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}} = m\bar L(\mu_\Theta)
    + (\sigma_0m/2)\min_\gamma\sum_{ij}\gamma_{ij}\norm{\vartheta_i - \vartheta^0_j}^2_{\Ginv}$ is the minimum over
    the finitely many vertices $\gamma$ of the transportation polytope of the smooth energies $J^\gamma_m$, so it is
    locally Lipschitz, the sum of a smooth function and a semiconcave one (a quadratic in $\Theta$ minus a maximum of
    linear functions), and its Clarke subdifferential at $\Theta$ is the convex hull of the gradients
    $\nabla J^\gamma_m(\Theta)$ of the active vertices. The vertex-selection solutions of
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$)} solve the differential inclusion
    $\dd\Theta/\dd s\in-G_m\partial J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}(\Theta)$; along them $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$ is non-increasing,
    with $\dd J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}/\dd s = -\norm{\nabla J^\gamma_m(\Theta)}^2_{G_m}$ wherever the plan $\gamma$ is
    unique, and at a tie the directional derivative along either active plan's drift is at most
    $-\norm{\nabla J^\gamma_m}^2_{G_m}$.
  \item \emph{(convergence)} $J_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$ is definable in the o-minimal structure $\R_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$ on
    the a priori ball --- finitely many compositions of polynomials, exponentials and the saturations $\tanh$ and
    $\operatorname{sig}$, a finite quadrature sum (or an analytic function of $\Theta$ for the period average of an
    analytic reference), and a minimum over finitely many such functions; for the finite quadrature it is definable in
    $\R_{\exp}$ on all of $\R^{mp}$, and for the period average of an analytic reference in $\R_{\mathrm{an}}$ on the
    ball --- so it satisfies the Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz inequality in the nonsmooth sense~\cite{S43}. The change of
    variables $Y = G_m^{-1/2}\Theta$ maps $\dd\Theta/\dd s\in-G_m\partial J$ into
    $\dd Y/\dd s\in-\partial(J\circ G_m^{1/2})(Y)$, the form in which the theorems of~\cite{S43} are stated;
    definable locally Lipschitz functions obey the chain rule along absolutely continuous curves, so along a
    subgradient trajectory $\dd J/\dd s = -\norm{\dd\Theta/\dd s}^2_{G_m^{-1}}$~\cite{S43}, and with the KL
    inequality every bounded subgradient trajectory has finite length and converges to a Clarke-critical point.
    Every vertex-selection and every generalised solution of \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^{\mathrm{OT}}$)} is such a
    trajectory, bounded by Step~0's a priori estimate with $\operatorname{conv}\supp\nu$; hence it converges, and so
    do the solutions of \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m^\nu$)} (a minimum over the Voronoi assignments) and of
    \textup{($\mathfrak{A}_m$)} (smooth: {\L}ojasiewicz's gradient inequality for real-analytic functions, in
    Kurdyka's form for definable ones~\cite{S44}).
  \item \emph{(the limit)} At a Clarke-critical point whose optimal plan is unique, the plan is a vertex and the
    point is a critical point of the frozen energy $J^\gamma_m$; for commensurate $m$ the plan is integral (an
    assignment $\pi$) and the limit is a critical point of the frozen energy $J^\pi_m$, every particle leaking toward
    its assigned atom --- $\Theta^*_m(q)$, the replication of another critical point of $\mathcal{G}_q$, or a split
    configuration. (iii) does not classify these limits: on the instance $\mathcal{G}_q$ has a second local minimum
    (fit error 0.37), whose replication to $m = 15$ has the diagonal plan as its unique optimal plan and is a saddle
    of $J^{\mathrm{OT}}_{15}$ --- one of its separation blocks is indefinite although $q\in W_{\mathrm{adm}}$, the
    admissible set being defined at the branch $\Theta^*(q)$ (referee round 13). A trajectory that enters the basin
    of $\Theta^*_m(q)$ converges to it: the stationary statement of Proposition D$'''$.12(iii) is reached dynamically
    from every initial condition in that basin, with the limit's masses the plan's masses $q$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}
\status{(i) proved (Danskin's theorem and the structure of a minimum of smooth functions; the energy decrease is

\begin{thm}{Theorem}{D$'''$.18}[Theorem J uniformly in $m$ at a replicated configuration]
Let \textup{(CL)} be the real closed loop of Theorem J with a leak whose targets are class-constant and locally
constant near the configurations considered (the $\sigma$-modification; the nearest-atom leak with every class atom
inside its Voronoi cell; the OT leak at $w = q$ with $\varepsilon < \Delta_\nu/2$, Proposition D$'''$.12(iii)), and
let $\mathcal{W}$ be a compact set of weight vectors with $\min_kw_k\ge w_{\min} > 0$ such that for every
$w\in\mathcal{W}$ the weighted stationary point $\Theta^*(w)$ of $\mathcal{G}_w$ is a non-degenerate minimum with
margins
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{coll}}(w) := \lambda_{\min}(H_w)\ge\lambda_0 > 0,\qquad
  \lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}(w) := \min_k\lambda_{\min}(\sigma_0I - R^G_k(w))\ge\lambda_0 > 0,
\]
$H_w$ the whitened, weighted collective block and $\sigma_0I - R^G_k(w)$ the separation blocks of Proposition
D$'''$.5(i) (so $\mathcal{W}\subset W_{\mathrm{adm}}$). For a configuration $\Theta = (\vartheta_1,\dots,\vartheta_m)$
with a class map $\pi_0$ whose class sizes are $n_k = mw_k$, $w\in\mathcal{W}$, write
$u_i := \vartheta_i - \vartheta^*_{\pi_0(i)}(w)$, $\bar u_k$ for the class means of $u$,
$u_i^\perp := u_i - \bar u_{\pi_0(i)}$, and
\begin{gather*}
  \tnorm{u} := \max\Bigl\{\Bigl(\sum_kw_k\norm{\bar u_k}^2_{\Ginv}\Bigr)^{1/2},\ \max_i\norm{u_i^\perp}_{\Ginv}\Bigr\},\\
  \text{so that}\qquad\max_i\norm{u_i}_{\Ginv}\le(1 + w_{\min}^{-1/2})\tnorm{u}\qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \tnorm{u}\le2\max_i\norm{u_i}_{\Ginv}
\end{gather*}
(the second constant is sharp up to the factor $1 - 1/\max_kn_k$). There are $k_0$, $\rho$, $C$, $\kappa$ depending

$mw\in\N^{K_0}$, every class map $\pi_0$ with those class sizes and every $k_e\ge k_0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item the lift of the weighted $K_0$-atom orbit $\Gamma_w$ (Corollary D$'''$.14) is the unique $T_0$-periodic orbit
    of \textup{(CL)} whose parameters satisfy
    $\sup_t\max_i\norm{\vartheta_i(t) - \vartheta^*_{\pi_0(i)}(w)}_{\Ginv}\le\rho/2$ and whose plant state stays in
    Theorem J's tube;
  \item every solution with $\max_i\norm{\vartheta_i(0) - \vartheta^*_{\pi_0(i)}(w)}_{\Ginv}\le\rho/2$ and
    $\norm{x(0) - x_m(0)}\le R/2$ converges to it, and with $u(t)$ the deviation from the lifted orbit at the times
    $t = jT_0$,
    \[
      \tnorm{u(jT_0)}\le C e^{-(1 - \kappa)\min\{\lambda_{\mathrm{coll}},\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}\}jT_0/k_e}
      \bigl(\tnorm{u(0)} + \norm{x(0) - x_p(0)}/k_e\bigr);

  \item its Floquet spectrum is Proposition D$'''$.15's --- the $K_0$-atom orbit's and each class's ghost exponents

\begin{proof}
Theorem J's proof with the norm $\tnorm{\cdot}$ in place of the unnormalised $\norm{\cdot}_{G_m^{-1}}$; what has to
be checked is that every constant of its Steps 1--6 is independent of $m$ in the new norm. Three structural facts do
it.

(1) \emph{The plant sees the configuration through the class means, up to second order in the separations.}
$\fhat_\Theta = \sum_kw_kn_k^{-1}\sum_{i\in k}\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\vartheta_i)$ and $\sum_{i\in k}u_i^\perp = 0$, so
\[
  \fhat_\Theta = \sum_kw_k\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\bar\vartheta_k)
  + \tfrac12\sum_kw_kn_k^{-1}\sum_{i\in k}\nabla^2\tilde\Phi(\cdot\,;\bar\vartheta_k)[u_i^\perp,u_i^\perp]
  + O(\max_i\norm{u_i^\perp}^3):
\]
the first-order variation of the residual $r_\Theta$, hence of the slaved error
$e_\Theta = \varepsilon r_\Theta(x_m) + O(\varepsilon^2)$ of Steps 1--2, is a function of the class means alone, and
the separations enter at second order with a coefficient bounded by $\sup\norm{\nabla^2\tilde\Phi}$, whatever $m$.
Steps 1--2 (the tube, the slaved error, the fast variable $\zeta$) involve $\Theta$ through $r_\Theta$ and --- in the
$\zeta$-forcing $-\partial_\Theta e_\Theta\cdot\dot\Theta$ and the remainder $\partial_eF\cdot\zeta$ --- through the
particles' drifts, which are $O(\varepsilon)$ per particle; by (1) and (2) their constants are uniform in $m$.

(2) \emph{Each particle's drift depends on the others only through the plant.} The particle law is
$\dot\vartheta_i = F(x,e,\vartheta_i) - \sigma(\vartheta_i - T_i)$: the right-hand side of particle $i$ is a function
of $(x,e,\vartheta_i)$ and of its target. So the frozen-$\Theta$ averaged drift
$\langle H\rangle_i(\Theta) = -G\nabla\mathcal{V}[\mu_\Theta](\vartheta_i)$ and the antiderivative $u_i(\Theta,t)$ of
Step~3 are functions of $(\vartheta_i,\fhat_\Theta)$, bounded and $C^2$ in $\vartheta_i$ with $m$-independent bounds,
and their derivatives in $\vartheta_j$ ($j\ne i$) are $O(1/m)$ and pass through $\fhat$ --- that is, through the class
means at first order. Writing the second-order remainder of the drift at the replicated configuration per particle,
$N_i(u) = O(\norm{u_i}^2) + O(\norm{u_i}\cdot(1/m)\sum_j\norm{u_j}) + O((1/m)\sum_j\norm{u_j}^2)$ (the second
derivatives $\partial^2_{\vartheta_i\vartheta_i}$ are $O(1)$, $\partial^2_{\vartheta_i\vartheta_j}$ and
$\partial^2_{\vartheta_j\vartheta_j}$ for $j\ne i$ are $O(1/m)$; $\partial^2_{\vartheta_j\vartheta_l}$ for distinct
$j,l\ne i$ vanishes in $\langle H\rangle$, whose residual is linear in the $\tilde\Phi$'s, and is $O(1/m^2)$ in the
second-order term $H_2$, which contains products of $\fhat$), so the remainder is bounded in $\tnorm{\cdot}$ by an
$m$-independent multiple of $\tnorm{u}^2$; the same holds for the near-identity transformations of Step~3 and for the
$O(\varepsilon^3)$ remainders of \textup{(N)}, which are built from the same functions.

(3) \emph{The linearisation at the replicated configuration is block diagonal with an $m$-independent spectrum, and
contracts in $\tnorm{\cdot}$.} By Proposition D$'''$.5(i) and (1)--(2), $D\langle H\rangle(\Theta^*_m(w))$ acts on each
separation $u_i^\perp$ of class $k$ separately, as the matrix $GR_k - \sigma_0I$ --- the same for every particle of
the class, similar to $-(\sigma_0I - R^G_k(w))$ whose eigenvalues are $\le-\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}$ --- and on the
class means as minus the collective block, self-adjoint in the weighted norm
$(\sum_kw_k\norm{\bar u_k}^2_{\Ginv})^{1/2}$ with eigenvalues $\ge\lambda_{\mathrm{coll}}$; the two families do not
mix. Hence $I + \varepsilon T_0D\langle H\rangle(\Theta^*_m(w))$ has $\tnorm{\cdot}$-operator norm
$1 - \varepsilon T_0\min\{\lambda_{\mathrm{coll}},\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}\}$ once $\varepsilon T_0$ times the largest
eigenvalue is below 1, the same bound for every $m$; by (2), $D\langle H\rangle$ is Lipschitz on the
$\tnorm{\cdot}$-ball around $\Theta^*_m(w)$ in that operator norm with an $m$-independent constant $C_N$, the
constant used below (the remainder of (2) is then at most $(C_N/2)\tnorm{u}^2$, by integration).

With (1)--(3), Step~4 runs as written in the norm $\tnorm{\cdot}$ for $P_Y$ ($P_\zeta$ is unchanged), giving the
Lipschitz constant $1 - \varepsilon T_0\min\{\lambda_{\mathrm{coll}},\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}}\}(1 - \kappa)$ of $P_Y$ on
the $\tnorm{\cdot}$-ball of radius $\rho$, $\kappa = O(\rho + \varepsilon)$, with $\rho := \min\{\lambda_0/(4C_N)$,
the cell and plan margins of the leak divided by $C(1 + w_{\min}^{-1/2})\}$ --- the $\tnorm{\cdot}$-ball of radius
$\rho$ reaches the per-particle distance $(1 + w_{\min}^{-1/2})\rho$, the solutions of (b) reach $C$ times that, and
the leak's targets must stay constant there; the coupling constants $\Lip_\zeta P_Y = O(\varepsilon)$ and
$\Lip_YP_\zeta = O(\varepsilon^3)$ are $m$-independent by (1) and (2). Step~6's $2\times2$ Lipschitz matrix then makes
$P$ a contraction in the adapted product norm on that ball, uniformly in $m$: its fixed point is unique there, and it
is the lift of $\Gamma_w$, which is a fixed point by Corollary D$'''$.14(i) and lies in the ball for $k_e\ge k_0$
because $\sup_t\tnorm{\vartheta^\Gamma(t) - \vartheta^*(w)} = O(1/k_e)$ with an $m$-independent constant (it is the
weighted $K_0$-atom distance, Theorem J(a) for \textup{(CL$_w$)}). This is (a) and (b): the per-particle ball of
radius $\rho/2$ lies in the $\tnorm{\cdot}$-ball of radius $\rho$ (since $\tnorm{u}\le2\max_i\norm{u_i}$), which
gives the uniqueness set of (a) and the start set of (b), and the $\tnorm{\cdot}$-ball of radius $\rho$ lies in the
per-particle ball of radius $(1 + w_{\min}^{-1/2})\rho$, which is what the margins have to cover. (c) is Proposition
D$'''$.15 with its averaging argument, the ghost matrices $A_k$ being $m$-independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{rmk}{Remark}{D$'''$.19}[which norm]
In the unnormalised norm $\norm{\cdot}_{G_m^{-1}}$ of Theorem J the basin radius $\rho_0$ is $m$-independent too ---
the Hessian of $J_m$ has the $m$-independent spectrum of (3) and its Lipschitz constant in that norm is $O(1)$ --- but
a ball of fixed radius in that norm contains only configurations whose particles are within $\rho_0/\sqrt m$ of their
atoms on average: it shrinks per particle. And Theorem J's threshold is not $m$-independent in that norm: the lifted
orbit sits $\sqrt m\cdot O(1/k_e)$ away from $\Theta^*_m(w)$ there ($\sqrt m$ times the weighted $K_0$-atom
distance), and so does the first averaging correction, so the implicit-function step needs $k_e\ge C\sqrt m/\rho_0$
--- Corollary D$'''$.14(ii)'s proviso, and the actual reason for the per-particle norm. That norm is the right one
because the dynamics is a mean-field one --- each particle feels its own position and the class means --- and it
cannot be weakened to a mean-square ($W_2$-type) ball for the nearest-atom leak: a single particle pushed into
another Voronoi cell changes the cluster weights, and the solution converges to the lift of different weights however
small its mean-square displacement --- for $m$ large enough that the shifted weights are admissible. At small $m$
they need not be (at $m = 15$ on the matching prior, one particle moved out of a class of five gives the weights
$(6,5,4)/15$, whose lift is a saddle, numerics below); the solution then splits instead, and either way it does not
converge to $\Gamma_q$. Under the OT leak the plan restores the masses (a transit, Proposition D$'''$.12(v)), and the
basin at the level of the empirical measure is larger: for the averaged loop Theorem D$'''$.21 below proves an
$m$-independent energy basin, which contains configurations with a fixed fraction of the particles in other cells.
\end{rmk}
\status{Proved as an adaptation of Theorem J's proof, at Theorem J's level of detail: Steps 1--3 and 5 carry over
with $m$-independent constants by the structural facts (1)--(2), and Steps 4 and 6 are rerun in the norm
$\tnorm{\cdot}$ with (3); the $m$-uniformity rests on the two-sided norm equivalence and on the mean-field structure
of every map built in Steps 1--4. The inputs are those of Theorem J, Corollary D$'''$.14(i), Proposition D$'''$.15,
the margins $\lambda_0$ of $\mathcal{W}$ and the leak's cell and plan margins. It replaces Corollary D$'''$.14(ii)'s
proviso by an $m$-uniform statement and settles the spectral and the basin part of Corollary D$'''$.7's uniformity
question in the per-particle norm. Checked: \code{tests/test_uniform_basin.py}, on the averaged loop at the prior
with centres shifted by $+0.15$ at the matching masses, where the residual is not zero and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{sep}} = 0.80\sigma_0$ (so the per-particle terms that vanish at the matching prior are exercised):
the second-order remainder of the drift is bounded by $C_N\tnorm{u}^2$ with $C_N < 0.1$ for random per-particle
displacements at $m = 6$, $30$, $150$, $600$, not growing with $m$; for a one-particle displacement the displaced
