### A.4 [minor] PARADIGM.md §2, Theorem A(ii), lines 117–122: "For any μ_0 ∈ P(Θ_K), the measure μ_t := (X_t)#μ_0 transported by the flow map X_t of the velocity field v_t(ϑ) := Proj_K(ϑ, G∇g_t(ϑ)) … μ_t is the Wasserstein gradient flow of the linear functional F_t"; §0 lines 32–33 (headline); §1 lines 67–68 (g_t, F_t defined through e(t), x(t)); §5 line 352 (Theorem D cites A(ii)).

CONFIRMED (all three reviewers). v_t is built from x(t) and e(t) = x(t) − x_m(t), and in closed loop ẋ = f + ẋ_m − k_e e − ∫Φ(x;·)dμ_t, so v_t is a functional of μ_t itself. For atomic μ_0 the pair (x, μ) is the finite-dimensional ODE (L) and the statement is fine; for general μ_0 — which (ii) explicitly covers — "the flow map of v_t" exists only once the closed-loop signal x(·) does, i.e. after Theorem D, which in turn cites A(ii) for the particle case. As written (ii) is either conditional on a prescribed x(·) or circular, and neither is said. Further, F_t depends on t through the closed-loop state, so "μ_t is the Wasserstein gradient flow of F_t" is a frozen-time identification of the velocity: no energy-dissipation identity or EVI for F_t(μ_t) follows, and linearity of F_t in μ is not absence of interaction (the interaction enters through e(t)); this matters for §8 branch 1's appeal to Chizat–Bach, whose results are for a fixed functional.

**Argument / counterexample.** Two closed-loop solutions from the same x_0 with different μ_0 have different e(·), hence different fields v_t; "the velocity field v_t" is not one object on P(Θ_K). F_t(μ_t) = −e(t)ᵀf̂(x(t)) changes sign whenever e crosses zero, so it is not monotone along the flow; the Lyapunov object is V_OT (Theorem B), not F_t. The identification itself checks out: the atomic Otto gradient of F_t = −(1/m)Σg_t(ϑ_i) at atom i is m·(−(1/m)∇g_t(ϑ_i)) = −∇g_t(ϑ_i), so the W_{G⁻¹} velocity is +G∇g_t, independent of m.

**Fix.** State (ii) for a given continuous signal t ↦ (x(t), e(t)) on [0,T] ("in particular the m-particle closed-loop solution, which exists by Cauchy–Lipschitz; for non-atomic μ_0 the closed loop is the system of Theorem D"); phrase the gradient claim as "at each t, v_t coincides on Θ_K^ε with minus the W_{G⁻¹}-gradient of the frozen functional F_t, i.e. (L) is the Lagrangian particle form of a non-autonomous Wasserstein gradient system whose potential is driven by the closed loop; no energy identity for F_t is claimed"; soften the §0 headline sentence accordingly.

### A.7 [minor] PARADIGM.md §2, proof of (ii), lines 138–141: "v_t is Lipschitz in ϑ on Θ_K (the operator Proj_K is Lipschitz in ϑ, ∇g_t is C¹), so the flow map exists and the push-forward solves the continuity equation in the distributional sense (Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Prop. 8.1.8)"; §1 lines 64–65: "Θ_K is forward invariant under ϑ̇ = Proj_K(ϑ, ·) (on the boundary the outward component is removed)".

CONFIRMED in substance (reviewers 1, 2, 3); the exact AGS lemma numbers the reviewers quote for the push-forward direction are UNCERTAIN, the substance is not. AGS Prop. 8.1.8 is the representation/uniqueness direction (a narrowly continuous solution of the continuity equation with ∫∫|v_t|dμ_t dt < ∞ and ∫_0^T (sup_B|v_t| + Lip(v_t,B)) dt < ∞ for compact B is the push-forward of μ_0 by the characteristics) and is stated for Borel fields on all of ℝ^d. The direction the proof needs — the push-forward by a Lipschitz flow solves the continuity equation — is the elementary chain-rule computation d/dt∫ϕdμ_t = ∫⟨∇ϕ, v_t⟩dμ_t of AGS §8.1, not Prop. 8.1.8. Forward invariance of Θ_K is asserted in §1 without citation; it is Nagumo's theorem for the closed convex set, and I checked the tangency: at ‖θ‖ = θ_max with θᵀy > 0, θᵀProj = (1−h)θᵀy = 0 since h = 1; on the s-faces the ramp gives velocity 0. **Argument / counterexample.** Not a counterexample to the conclusion (which is true) but a citation whose hypotheses are not met as written and which asserts the converse of what is used.

**Fix.** Write: "Extend v_t to ℝ^p by v_t∘P_{Θ_K} (P_{Θ_K} the metric projection onto the convex compact Θ_K, 1-Lipschitz); then sup_{t≤T} Lip(v_t) ≤ C(X, Θ_K, G, sup_t‖e(t)‖) < ∞, the flow exists (Cauchy–Lipschitz), Θ_K is forward invariant by Nagumo's theorem since Proj_K(ϑ,·) lies in the tangent cone at every boundary point, (X_t)#μ_0 solves the continuity equation by the chain rule (AGS §8.1), and it is the unique narrowly continuous solution by AGS Prop. 8.1.8."
