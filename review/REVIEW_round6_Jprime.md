## ISSUES

### 1. (minor) Step 3 / Step 4 / statement: the expansion needs five derivatives of ψ_φ, not four; the stated class φ ∈ C² is not what the proof covers
Location: statement of Theorem J′ ("for every observable φ ∈ C²(ℝ^{mp}) with polynomially bounded derivatives up to order two", l. 2998–3000); Step 3 ("Let φ ∈ C⁴ … up to order four", l. 3086; "… through B₀ψ₂ and B₀ψ₃, of φ up to order 4", l. 3108–3110); Step 4 ("give ψ_φ ∈ C⁴", l. 3120–3121).
What is wrong: ψ₂′ is the θ-antiderivative of (among others) (B₀ψ₁)|_{η*} = −H·∇(u·∇φ) − τ₀tr(G_m∇²(u·∇φ)), whose second term contains ∇³φ. So ψ₂ is of order 3 in φ, and the order-ε² term B₀ψ₂ ∋ τ₀tr(G_m∇²ψ₂′) contains ∇⁵φ. R_ε is therefore polynomially bounded only if ψ_φ ∈ C⁵ with polynomially bounded derivatives up to order five (ψ₃ is of order 2 in φ, so B₀ψ₃ is of order 4, as stated — the undercount is in B₀ψ₂). Interior Schauder theory gains exactly two derivatives plus Hölder: ψ_φ ∈ C^{5,α} needs φ ∈ C^{3,α}, and even the text's own "ψ_φ ∈ C⁴" needs φ ∈ C^{2,α}, not C² (for φ merely C², ψ_φ ∈ W^{4,p}_loc ⊂ C^{3,β} only). The constant C_φ in (b)–(c) therefore depends on derivatives of φ up to order 3 (Hölder), and the theorem is not proved for a general C² observable with the stated constant.
Fix: state (b)–(e) for φ ∈ C³ with Hölder-continuous third derivatives (or simply C^∞) and polynomially bounded derivatives up to order three; in Step 3 write C⁵ / "up to order five" and in Step 4 "ψ_φ ∈ C^{5,α}". Every observable used anywhere (Θ_k, Θ_kΘ_l, ‖η‖², Θ∧V, b·∇ψ) is smooth, so nothing downstream changes. (For (b) alone, ψ_φ ∈ C³ suffices, since one may drop ψ₂′: this is why the application of (b) to b·∇ψ_φ in Step 5 is fine with ψ_φ ∈ C⁴.)

### 2. (cosmetic) w_φ is written for a diagonal G
Location: Step 3, definition of w_φ := Σ_{i,l}G_l ∂_{ϑ_l}Φ̃(x_m(θ);ϑ_i)∂_{(i,l)}φ (l. 3087). For a general symmetric G the η-coefficient of the B₀-drift contracted with ∇φ is (w_φ)_k = Σ_i (∇_ϑΦ̃_k(x_m;ϑ_i))ᵀG∇_{ϑ_i}φ. Step 7 explicitly allows a symmetric non-diagonal G ("for a symmetric G_m"). Fix: write w_φ with G as a matrix, or state once that G is diagonal (as in every instance of the repository).

### 3. (cosmetic) Proposition J′.2 (i)/(ii) wording
(i) "its stationary density is m^{(1)}_ε = π(1 + εg) + O(ε²)" is justified by Steps 4–5 only in the weak sense (against observables with polynomially bounded derivatives), not as a pointwise statement about densities; the current formula and the EPR formula use only the weak form plus the formal FP expansion, so nothing is lost by saying so. (ii) "the first-order averaged dynamics is the Langevin dynamics of J_m + εJ₁ up to O(ε²)": when b = −G_m∇J₁ the first-order averaged SDE *is* the Langevin dynamics of J_m + εJ₁ exactly, and its stationary density is exactly ∝ exp(−(J_m + εJ₁)/τ₀); "up to O(ε²)" is weaker than what holds.

### 4. (minor, numerics) The batch-mean standard errors of `simulate_stationary` are underestimated by a factor 1.4–1.9
Location: `simulate_stationary` (`n_batches=20`, `T_slow=40` ⇒ batch length 2 slow units), used by `exp_dither_stationary.py` and `test_dither.py`.
What is wrong: the spectral gap of L̄₀ on this instance is λ₁ = 0.286 per slow unit (eigenvalues 0, 0.286, 0.758, 0.937, 1.206, …, computed from the grid operator), i.e. the slowest relaxation time is 3.5 slow units, longer than a batch. Adjacent batch means are correlated (ρ ≈ e^{−2λ₁} = 0.56 for the slowest mode), so `batch.std(ddof=1)/√20` is biased low. With the spectral weights of each observable (`se_bias.py`): true Var(mean)/naive estimate = 2.99 (α), 3.53 (γ), 2.11 (α²), 1.94 (γ²), 2.05 (αγ), 2.83 (2L̄), i.e. the quoted standard errors should be multiplied by 1.73, 1.88, 1.45, 1.39, 1.43, 1.68 respectively. (A partially compensating inflation from the deterministic phase offsets between batches — Issue 5 — is ≈10 % at k_e = 10 and negligible at k_e ≥ 20.) Every "(measured − predicted)/s.e." and "within the quoted standard errors" statement in `results/dither/table.md` inherits this.
Fix: the K replicas are independent, so compute the standard error from the between-replica (or replica-group) variance of the per-replica time averages, which is unbiased; or use ≥ 5 batches of ≥ 8 slow units (≥ 2 relaxation times).

### 5. (minor, numerics) The averaging window and the batches are not whole numbers of reference periods
Location: `simulate_stationary`: `n_avg = round(T_slow·k_e/h)` with `h = T₀/n_per`, so `n_avg/n_per = T_slow·k_e/T₀` = 63.66, 127.33, 254.65, 509.30 periods at k_e = 10, 20, 40, 80; the observables are accumulated at every step, so the phase average m_ε requires is realised only up to the leftover fraction of a period.
Consequence (first order, Gibbs-averaged, `mc_diagnostics.py` (B)): the bias on E[α], E[γ] is ε·E_π[u(θ)] weighted by the leftover phases: +7.8·10⁻⁵, −3.1·10⁻⁵, −4.8·10⁻⁷, −1.8·10⁻⁶ (α) at k_e = 10…80 — negligible against the standard errors. For the circulation observable Θ∧V, whose θ-dependent part Θ∧u̇ is O(1) (E_π amplitude 0.29), the bias is +1.7·10⁻⁴, −8.5·10⁻⁵, +1.5·10⁻⁴, −1.9·10⁻⁵ at k_e = 10…80, which is of the same size as the predicted Λ_ε = ε·(−0.0051) = −5.1·10⁻⁴, −2.5·10⁻⁴, −1.3·10⁻⁴, −6.3·10⁻⁵ (orientation +1). Table 4 can therefore not be a consistency check at the level of the prediction even with infinitely many replicas.
Fix: choose `n_avg` (and the batch length) as integer multiples of `n_per` (e.g. `n_avg = n_per·round(T_slow·k_e/T₀)`).

### 6. (minor, numerics) Burn-in of 4 slow units ≈ 1.1 relaxation times: ≈3 % of the initial O(ε) offset survives in the window average
Location: `exp_dither_stationary.py` `BURN = 4.0`; replicas start from π, which differs from m_ε by O(ε).
What is wrong: for the slowest mode the fraction of the initial offset that survives burn-in and is then averaged over the window is e^{−λ₁B}(1 − e^{−λ₁T})/(λ₁T) = 0.028. The measured first-order shift m_ε(φ) − π(φ) is thus biased toward π by ≈2.8 % of itself (for the part of φ living in the slowest mode: 50 % of Var(α), 80 % of Var(γ)), a systematic effect of the same size as the "few percent" agreement the text claims for the orientation-odd part (Remark J′.1, Status paragraph), and in the direction of under-measuring the shift.
Fix: burn-in ≥ 15 slow units, or start the replicas from the first-order law π(1 + εg) (then the transient is O(ε²)).

### 7. (cosmetic) Table 1's "resolved" criterion and fitted slopes
Location: `exp_dither_stationary.py` l. 139–142: points with |m_ε − first order| > 2 s.e. enter the slope fit of log|m_ε − first order|. With the s.e. of Issue 4 the criterion is ≈1.1–1.4 true s.e., so the fitted "second-order slope" is dominated by noise unless the residuals are many s.e. ### 8. (cosmetic) Proposition J′.2(iv), "for any f, x_m, G, σ₀"
b_fr does not depend on σ₀ and depends on G only through the factor G, so the quantifier is fine but idle; and the argument uses Theorem I's setting (saturated Gaussian features, f ∈ C²), which should be recalled. The chain itself is sound: the (i,j) block of the antisymmetric part is (1/(2m))∇∇′A_{μ_Θ}(ϑ_i,ϑ_j) with the kernel arguments differentiated and μ_Θ frozen; A_μ = A_f + B(f̂_μ) with B linear (Theorem I(ii), r_μ affine in f̂_μ); varying the third atom isolates ∇∇′B(Φ̃(·;ϑ₃))(ϑ,ϑ′), which is ϑ₃-independent and vanishes at the admissible zero feature (α = 0); an antisymmetric kernel with ∇∇′A ≡ 0 on the connected ℝ^p × ℝ^p is a(ϑ) − a(ϑ′), a coboundary, hence zero by Theorem I(i), for every μ, contradicting Theorem I(vi). The gain-scale claim rests on b_fr ∝ t, b_osc ∝ t² under G → tG, which holds because the leak drops out of u (⟨u⟩ = 0): confirmed numerically (ratios exactly 0.5/0.25 and 3/9).
