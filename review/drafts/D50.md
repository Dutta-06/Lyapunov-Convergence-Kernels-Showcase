(i) *(the Gibbs law and its exact identity)* ∇F(ζ) = Φw − y, so dζ = −∇F(ζ)dt/N + (2T/N)^{1/2}dW — an overdamped Langevin diffusion at

    Cov_π(Φw) = T·ΦE_π[D]Φᵀ   at every noise level;

(ii) *(fluctuation–dissipation on a support)* Let S be a support on which Φ_S has full column rank. By (i)(b),

(iii) *(the rich limit; formal)* For masses εb, as ε → 0 at fixed σ, the dual's law in ν = ζ/Λ(ε) concentrates on the optimal face of

The face is crossed only on the time Λ²N²/σ², and the coefficients do not see where on it the dual is, except at the face's boundary,
where an off-support coefficient reaches O(T^{3/2}) while its curve's small-α branch holds (β_j ~ γmT ≪ 2c), O(1) beyond.

(iv) *(the noisy transit)* In τ = t/Λ the noise enters with the intensity κ = σ/(NΛ^{1/2}) ∝ σε^{1/4}: dν = (y − Φw)dτ/N + κdW_τ. The

two runs have different active sets, and the entering kernel's finite-ε absorption time O(N/(Λ·D_j|P_{T⁻}φ_j|²)) ∝ ε^{1/2}, within which

against the gaps between them; the transit converges to the inverse scale space at the rate σε^{1/4} (half the noiseless ε^{1/2}) — the
price of parsimony, a transit time ∝ ε^{−1/2}, buys the averaging of the noise.
