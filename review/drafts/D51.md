(ii) *(the near-tie law)* Let a kernel e enter at τ_e on the path while a kernel r near the same feature, not between e and the feature,

the entry fits, s_A its sign); the runner-up's share 𝒮(x) = |w_r|/(|w_e| + |w_r|) of the feature is the problem's end. At a symmetric tie

At a finite margin the next order enters, and at the margins where the threshold binds (μ* ~ 10⁻³–10⁻¹) it is not small. (a) The

(iii) *(the creep)* A kernel left inside its wall at the margin μ beside a feature not yet fitted (⟨r∂_cφ⟩ = g over a time T in τ)
creeps by δc ≈ (2a²b^{3/2}/(π²c))εμ⁻³gT (D‴.48(iii)(a); the core's profile, which overstates 𝒲 inside ξ ≲ 30 by (i)) and its dual gains

ε ≳ (π²c/(2a²b^{3/2}))μ³D/(gT). These are scales: g, T and μ vary along the path, and `creep_reaches` integrates the creep along the
path's residuals.

(iv) *(an estimate of the mass)* Hence an estimate, from the path alone, of the largest mass at which the loop forms exactly the sliding
path's kernels: ε_c = min(ε_entry, ε_creep) (`recovery_threshold`; equal masses). At the entry of i a kernel j that never enters is taken
to form iff its margin μ_j(τ_i) < ε^{1/2}x_ij, ε_entry = min (μ_j(τ_i)/x_ij)² over the pairs with x_ij > 0: x_ij = Δ_ij + ξ_θ, Δ_ij the
pull on j measured on the entry's ε-free inner problem — the path's active block and the entrant on the critical curve in s, the entrant
from far behind its wall, the kernels that never enter as tracers whose duals the residual moves (`entry_pull`) — and 𝒲(ξ_θ) = θ; for a

the next order of (ii) biases it where it binds: the near-tie partner's threshold, posed at the symmetric configuration, misses the
partners' differential approach creep ((ii)(a)), so the loop fails below ε_c, the more so the larger the margin; a loser is treated as

with ε_loop/ε_c of order one, as in (iv), the loop's own follow the same laws shifted in log ε — they were not measured.
