with the masses ε on a grid of centres; r = f − f̂.

(i) *(the exact averaged loop)* With the parameters frozen and k_e > Lip(r), the loop ė = −k_ee + r(x_m + e) has a unique, exponentially
stable T-periodic solution e_p, the fixed point of the contraction e ↦ L[r(x_m + e)] on the T-periodic functions (L = (d/dt + k_e)⁻¹, factor
Lip(r)/k_e); the slow parameters see the period averages along the frozen orbit x_p = x_m + e_p: the averaged loop is the free law with
moving centres driven by g_i = ⟨φ_i(x_p)e_p⟩, h_i = ⟨∂_cφ_i(x_p)e_p⟩ (`FrozenOrbit`) — a drive that is not a gradient (Theorem I), so the
transit's structure is D‴.48(iii)'s with this drive, formally: kernels enter one at a time on the scale Λ(ε), those that have not entered
stay where they started, the entered ones slide to a zero of the averaged drive — an exact fit of f on the orbit's range where f is
representable there. Its leading order in 1/k_e is the bilinear form ⟨φ_i, r⟩_L along the reference (`OrbitBumps`), with an additive error
O(|r|²/(k_e²σ)) that is not small against the drives' differences at near ties: which grid kernel forms a feature is the frozen orbit's
choice, not the reference's.

(ii) *(the real loop)* The real loop follows the exact averaged loop's transit — numerically: the same entrants, coefficients and inactive
displacements, entry times within 0.4 of the averaged loop's (0.2%) at ε = 10⁻⁴ … 10⁻⁶, where the leading order is 7% off and picks the
neighbouring kernels. The active block is not slow — its centres relax 5–13 times faster than the reference's frequency and swing within
each period —, so averaging is a statement about the inactive kernels' duals; the active block's agreement is numerical. At masses small
against the entries' margins (D‴.51) the controller forms one kernel per feature of f in the orbit's range, at the feature's centre and
amplitude, and then tracks exactly; at a finite ε near ties along the orbit decide the count (at ε = 10⁻⁵: five kernels at k_e = 20, four at
ω = 1 and with a quasi-periodic reference, the two loops alike).

(iii) *(where it fails)* (a) Low gain: below k_e ≈ 4 formed kernels are expelled from the range — the orbit's error, O(|r|/k_e), carries the
state beyond the reference's range during the transit, kernels formed there are stranded at |x| ≈ 1.5 with |w| = 0.51–0.82 once the error
shrinks (six kernels at k_e = 2 and 3, three of them beyond [−1.2, 1.2]). (b) Measurement noise without a leak: 1% of the reference's

tracking error at its unadapted level), so the leak's hold and its bias are tied — and every leak fails at 2%. (c) Features near the range:
a feature within ≈ 1σ of the range is formed; at ≈ 1.6σ it is not, and the in-range fit is distorted by a compensating pair; beyond ≈ 3σ it
is not formed within the horizon and the in-range fit is clean (Part C: at 0.92σ formed at τ ≈ 1.2·10³; at 1.6σ a pair of coefficients
−1.51, 1.21 near 0.15; at 2.9σ and 3.9σ the in-range fit within 0.003 of the true amplitudes).

(iv) *(the price)* The transit takes t ≈ k_eΛ(ε)τ₁ for large gains (τ₁/k_e = 4.9 at k_e = 2, 12.7–13.9 from k_e = 4 to 40): a smaller mass
forms fewer kernels and takes longer, the tracking error staying at its unadapted level until the first entry; a Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao
metric on the positions forms several kernels per feature at a finite ε (7 at 10⁻⁶, 3 at 10⁻⁸), and fixed centres leave the grid's residual

*Proof sketch.* (i) The map e ↦ L[r(x_m + e)] is a contraction of the continuous T-periodic functions in the sup norm, ‖L‖ = 1/k_e, so e_p
exists uniquely, and any solution with frozen parameters converges to it at the rate k_e − Lip(r). The slow parameters' rates are O(e·φ)
times the mobilities, small for the inactive kernels (their masses), so classical averaging (Bogoliubov; Sastry–Bodson) gives their duals'
averaged drives, period by period, at the frozen orbit. Expanding e_p = L[r(x_m)] + O(|r||r′|/k_e²) and φ_i(x_m + e_p) = φ_i(x_m) +
O(|e_p|/σ) gives the leading order and its additive error. ∎
