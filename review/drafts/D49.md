**Proposition D‴.49 (the rich transit in the closed loop: along the frozen periodic orbit the averaged loop is the free law driven by the
orbit's exact averaged drive; from small masses the adaptive controller forms the kernels of the nonlinearity's features that the orbit
visits, one at a time — under conditions on the gain against the active block's rates, on the noise and on the features near the

f̂ = Σ_iw_iφ(·; c_i), the kernels adapting by the paradigm's free law with moving centres (Proposition D‴.48(iii)) driven by the error —

In (ii)–(iv) f is a sum of Gaussians of the kernels' width (a wider feature takes several kernels and leaves a residual: D‴.58).

(i) *(the exact averaged loop)* With the parameters frozen and k_e > Lip(r), the loop ė = −k_ee + r(x_m + e) has a unique, exponentially
stable T-periodic solution e_p, the fixed point of the contraction e ↦ L[r(x_m + e)] on the T-periodic functions (L = (d/dt + k_e)⁻¹, factor
Lip(r)/k_e); the slow parameters see the period averages along the frozen orbit x_p = x_m + e_p: the averaged loop is the free law with
moving centres driven by g_i = ⟨φ_i(x_p)e_p⟩, h_i = ⟨∂_cφ_i(x_p)e_p⟩ (`FrozenOrbit`) — a drive that is not a gradient (Theorem I), so the
transit's structure is D‴.48(iii)'s with this drive, formally: kernels enter one at a time on the scale Λ(ε), those that have not entered
stay where they started, the entered ones slide to a zero of the averaged drive — an exact fit of f on the orbit's range where f is
representable there. Its leading order in 1/k_e is the bilinear form ⟨φ_i, r⟩_L along the reference (`OrbitBumps`), with an additive error
O(|r|²/(k_e²σ)) that is not small against the drives' differences at near ties: which grid kernel forms a feature is the frozen orbit's
choice, not the reference's. The averaging is that of the slow, inactive kernels; the active block (the formed kernels) is slow only while
its own rates — those of its linearisation in t — are below the error loop's k_e: a frozen-system statement in the sense of the classical
averaging of adaptive systems, the active block's rates entering through the fast subsystem (e and the active block together).

(ii) *(the real loop)* Where k_e > Lip(f) and the active block's rates are below k_e, the real loop follows the exact averaged loop's
transit — numerically: the same entrants, entry times within 0.4 of the averaged loop's (0.2%) at ε = 10⁻⁵ … 10⁻⁶ and within 2.5 over the
controller's knobs, the same coefficients summed over coinciding kernels (the split between coinciding kernels is a gauge the two loops
fix differently at ε = 10⁻⁴), where the leading order is 7% off and picks the neighbouring kernels. At masses small against the entries'
margins (D‴.51) the controller forms one kernel per feature of f in the orbit's range, at the feature's centre and amplitude, and then
tracks exactly; at a finite ε near ties along the orbit decide the count (at ε = 10⁻⁵: five kernels at k_e = 20 and ω = 1, four with a
quasi-periodic reference — for which no frozen periodic orbit exists and the averaged loop is not computed —, the two loops alike where
both exist).

(iii) *(where it fails)* (a) Low gain — an averaging failure of the fast active block, not of the transit: at k_e = 2 and 3 the formed
kernels' centres swing within one period by 0.42 and 0.18 during the transit (by 0.05 at k_e = 10), the active block's fastest rate is
4.5 and 2.4 times k_e (1.5 at k_e = 4, 0.27 at 10), and a kernel formed at an edge feature is carried out of the reference's range within
one period while the state itself stays in it (|x| ≤ 1.03) — kernel 17, formed at 0.70 with |w| = 0.82, from 0.60 to 1.24 in the period
before it leaves; once out it no longer sees the data and is stranded with its coefficient. The exact averaged loop, which sees only the
period's mean drive, expels nothing. The small parameter of this failure is not ε: expulsion persists and worsens as ε shrinks (1, 3, 3, 6
kernels expelled at ε = 10⁻⁴ … 10⁻⁷, k_e = 2), and the threshold in k_e moves with the features' amplitudes (f × 2 expels at k_e = 4,
f × ½ is clean at k_e = 2). In this instance the loop is clean for k_e ≥ 4, where the active block's fastest rate is at most 1.5k_e.
(b) Measurement noise: without a remedy 1% of the reference's amplitude expels 14 kernels and leaves the tracking error at its unadapted
level. The desired regressor x_m of D‴.31 (D‴.33) keeps the three features' kernels at 1% (the in-range fit's rms error 0.059, the true

formed late, through a cluster that compensates on the way (the given signs) or cleanly (the neighbour's sign flipped); beyond ≈ 3σ it is not
formed within τ = 2·10⁴ and the in-range fit is clean.

*Proof sketch.* (i) The map e ↦ L[r(x_m + e)] is a contraction of the continuous T-periodic functions in the sup norm, ‖L‖ = 1/k_e, so e_p
exists uniquely, and any solution with frozen parameters converges to it at the rate k_e − Lip(r). The inactive kernels' rates are O(e·φ)
times their mobilities, small with their masses, so classical averaging (Bogoliubov; Sastry–Bodson) gives their duals' averaged drives,
period by period, at the frozen orbit. The active block is not slow in ε: its rates are those of the formed coefficients' and centres'
mobilities, independent of the masses; averaging it needs the fast subsystem — e and the active block together — to converge to its
periodic state within a period, i.e. the active block's rates below the error loop's (the rest condition, checked numerically). Expanding
e_p = L[r(x_m)] + O(|r||r′|/k_e²) and φ_i(x_m + e_p) = φ_i(x_m) + O(|e_p|/σ) gives the leading order and its additive error. ∎
