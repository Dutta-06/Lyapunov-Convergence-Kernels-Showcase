**Proposition D‴.59 (the transit with moving widths: one Gaussian feature, one kernel, at any width; coinciding pairs, cancellations
and the dipole).**
Let each kernel carry its own width, θ_i = (c_i, σ_i), φ(x; θ_i) = exp(−(x − c_i)²/(2σ_i²)), the paradigm's law moving the width
beside the centre (σ̇_i = Gβ_ia_i⟨r∂_σφ_i⟩, ċ_i = Gβ_ia_i⟨r∂_cφ_i⟩); the sliding path, its blocks and the loop are D‴.48–D‴.53's
with θ in place of c (`moving_width.BumpsCW` in `rich_transit_nd`).

(i) *(one feature, in the continuum: no spurious minimum)* For a Gaussian feature Aφ(·; c*, σ*), the one-kernel energy with the
coefficient at its least-squares value,

    J(c, σ) = ½‖f*‖² − ½⟨f*, φ_{c,σ}⟩²/‖φ_{c,σ}‖² = ½A²π^{1/2}(σ* − 2σσ*²e^{−(c−c*)²/v}/v),   v = σ² + σ*²,

has one critical point, its global minimum J(c*, σ*) = 0, and tends to ½‖f*‖² as σ → 0, σ → ∞ or |c − c*| → ∞. So the transit's
first block on one Gaussian feature relaxes onto it exactly, whatever the width ratio, from any start whose coefficient has the
feature's sign (on the free law's curve a coefficient keeps its sign): one kernel, where fixed widths need several (D‴.58's quadratures
for wider features, stiff blocks for narrower ones). On samples J is J_N, whose limits as σ → 0 or ∞ fall below ½⟨f*²⟩ (a width
shrinking onto one sample, or growing past the window), so the statement is the continuum's; on the samples it is checked through the
paths (A).

(ii) *(several features)* On C's random three-feature targets — separations Δ ≥ 0.4 or 0.6, widths in [0.05, 0.3], amplitudes of
both signs — the path ends exact on 71 of 80, and otherwise in one of the following; a fourth, the cancellation, appears when features
are closer than the kernels' starting width.
(a) *A coinciding pair*: two kernels at one θ share a feature — on C's targets a kernel of an earlier block collapsing at the last
block onto a feature already fitted (by a kernel of either sign: a split, or a cancelling pair), on an exactly symmetric target the
near tie (D, d = 0). The fit is exact with one kernel too many; at coincidence the split between the two is a gauge. A pair a little
apart, Δc, fits one Gaussian to O(Δc⁴): the common width absorbs the second moment and the width split
σ_j − σ_i = (w_j − w_i)Δc²/(6Wσ) (W = w_i + w_j) the third.
(b) *A cancellation*: two features of one sign closer than the starting width σ₀ can be fitted with kernels of both signs — several
negative kernels along the path and at the end, coefficients beyond the amplitudes, the fit exact (H). A target of one sign does not
keep its kernels of one sign.
(c) *The dipole*: the family of sums of Gaussians with free θ is not closed — its closure holds the dipoles p·∇_θφ, the limits of two
opposite-sign kernels coalescing with |w| → ∞ at a fixed moment p = wΔθ (and, for k kernels coalescing, the (k − 1)-th derivatives) —,
and a block whose flow approaches an infimum attained only by a dipole runs off: two opposite-sign kernels coalesce, and with
v = |w_a − w_b|/2, μ₀ = 4Gc/A and K = v²(E − E_inf),

    v³ = 3μ₀K·s   (s the block's fast time),   so |w| ∝ s^{1/3}, |Δθ| ∝ s^{−1/3}, E − E_inf ∝ s^{−2/3},

the residual tending to the unattained infimum, the path stalled. In the real loop at a finite mass the dipole is transient: the pair
migrates toward a feature, its separation and the residual falling algebraically in τ, and lands on it as a cancelling coinciding pair
— an exact fit with one kernel too many and coefficients several times the amplitudes —; when, and on which feature, depends on ε.

(iii) *(under noise; the real loop)* Stopped by the discrepancy rule (the first block whose residual's rms is within (1 + δ)s), the
moving-width path's fit is the least-squares fit of the data started at the truth — a coinciding pair adding a noise kernel, a dipole
never reaching the discrepancy —; and the real loop at a small mass forms the path's kernels where the path ends exact.

*Proof.* (i) ⟨f*, φ_{c,σ}⟩ = A(2π)^{1/2}σσ*v^{−1/2}e^{−d²/(2v)} (d = c − c*) and ‖φ_σ‖² = π^{1/2}σ, so J is as stated and
∂_cJ = (2d/v)E₁, ∂_σJ = −(1/σ − 2σ/v + 2σd²/v²)E₁ with E₁ = π^{1/2}A²σσ*²e^{−d²/v}/v > 0 (`one_kernel_grad`). ∂_cJ = 0 forces d = 0;
there ∂_σJ = −π^{1/2}A²σ*²(σ*² − σ²)/v² vanishes only at σ = σ*, where J = ½A²π^{1/2}(σ* − σ*) = 0. As σ → 0 or ∞ or |d| → ∞ the
overlap term vanishes. The block's flow is a gradient flow of the analytic energy E(w, c, σ) ≥ J(c, σ) in the free law's metric (Lemma
D‴.53a); along it E decreases from ½‖f*‖², so on the flow J < ½‖f*‖² − η for some η > 0 once the first entry is made, which keeps
(c, σ) in a compact set of (ℝ × (0, ∞)) and |w| = |⟨f*, φ⟩|/‖φ‖² + O(E − J) bounded away from 0 and ∞ (as in Lemma D‴.53a(ii)); on that
set the metric is non-degenerate and the flow converges (Łojasiewicz) to a critical point of E, i.e. (w at its least-squares value) of
J: the minimum, the residual vanishing, so no kernel enters again. (ii)(a) For a pair w_i, w_j at c̄ − (w_j/W)Δc and c̄ + (w_i/W)Δc
(c̄ the centre of mass) with the widths σ_i, σ_j about σ, Σ_kw_kφ(θ_k) = Wφ + Σ_kw_k(σ_k − σ)∂_σφ + ½(w_iw_j/W)Δc²∂²_cφ +

the third is, up to a shift of the centre, cancelled by σ_j − σ_i = (w_j − w_i)Δc²/(6Wσ): one Gaussian to O(Δc⁴) for any split, a
cancelling pair (w_iw_j < 0) included. (b) numerical (H). (c) w_aφ(θ̄ + Δθ/2) + w_bφ(θ̄ − Δθ/2) = (w_a + w_b)φ(θ̄) + ½(w_a − w_b)Δθ·∇φ +
