**Proposition D‴.59 (the transit with moving widths: one kernel per Gaussian feature at any width; coinciding pairs and the dipole).**
Let each kernel carry its own width, θ_i = (c_i, σ_i), φ(x; θ_i) = exp(−(x − c_i)²/(2σ_i²)), the paradigm's law moving the width
beside the centre (σ̇_i = Gβ_ia_i⟨r∂_σφ_i⟩, ċ_i = Gβ_ia_i⟨r∂_cφ_i⟩); the sliding path, its blocks and the loop are D‴.48–D‴.53's
with θ in place of c (`moving_width.BumpsCW` in `rich_transit_nd`).

(i) *(one feature: no spurious minimum)* For a Gaussian feature Aφ(·; c*, σ*) in the continuum, the one-kernel energy with the
coefficient at its least-squares value,

    J(c, σ) = ½‖f*‖² − ½⟨f*, φ_{c,σ}⟩²/‖φ_{c,σ}‖² = ½A²π^{1/2}(σ* − 2σσ*²e^{−(c−c*)²/v}/v),   v = σ² + σ*²,

has one critical point, its global minimum J(c*, σ*) = 0, and tends to ½‖f*‖² as σ → 0, σ → ∞ or |c − c*| → ∞. So the transit's
first block on an isolated feature relaxes onto it exactly, whatever the width ratio: one kernel per feature, where fixed widths need
several (D‴.58's quadratures for wider features, stiff blocks for narrower ones).

(ii) *(several features: coinciding pairs and dipoles)* On separated features the path ends exact or in one of two configurations.
(a) *A coinciding pair*: two kernels at one (c, σ) share a feature — same-sign (a split: D‴.54's near tie with moving widths; a pair a
little apart fits one Gaussian to a higher order in its separation, the widths absorbing the second moment) or of opposite signs (a
cancelling pair); the fit is exact with one kernel too many and the split is a gauge. (b) *The dipole*: the family of sums of Gaussians with free (c, σ) is not
closed — its closure holds the dipoles p·∇_θφ, the limits of two opposite-sign kernels coalescing with |w| → ∞ at a fixed moment
p = wΔθ — and where the residual is better fitted by a dipole than by any finite pair, the block's fit has no minimiser and its flow
runs off: two opposite-sign kernels coalesce with

    |w| ∝ s^{1/3},   |Δθ| ∝ s^{−1/3}   (s the block's fast time),

the residual tending to an unattained infimum, the path stalled. Formally: at a fixed moment the pair's energy exceeds the dipole's
by κΔθ² = κp²/w² (κ > 0, from the second- and third-order Taylor terms of the pair about its midpoint), and on the free law's
exponential branch the coefficient's mobility grows like the coefficient, μ = Gβa′w′ → (4Gc/A)|w| (here 0.1|w|, already at
|w| ≈ 0.6–0.9), so along the slow manifold of the moment dw/ds ≈ 2μκp²/w³ ∝ w^{−2}. In the real loop at a finite mass the dipole is
transient: once the other features are fitted it lands on a feature as a cancelling coinciding pair — an exact fit with one kernel
too many and coefficients several times the amplitudes. Dipoles need opposite-sign kernels: a target of one sign cannot make them.

(iii) *(under noise; the real loop)* Stopped by the discrepancy rule (the first block whose residual's rms is within (1 + δ)s), the
moving-width path reaches the oracle error s(3K/N)^{1/2} of a K-feature fit with three parameters per kernel; and the real loop at a
small mass forms the path's kernels.

*Proof.* (i) ⟨f*, φ_{c,σ}⟩ = A(2π)^{1/2}σσ*v^{−1/2}e^{−d²/(2v)} (d = c − c*) and ‖φ_σ‖² = π^{1/2}σ, so J is as stated and

there ∂_σJ = −π^{1/2}A²σ*²(σ*² − σ²)/v² vanishes only at σ = σ*, where J = ½A²π^{1/2}(σ* − σ*) = 0. As σ → 0 or ∞ or |d| → ∞ the
overlap term vanishes. The block's flow is a gradient flow of the analytic energy in the free law's metric (Lemma D‴.53a), so it
converges (Łojasiewicz; the sublevel sets below ½‖f*‖² are compact in (c, σ), J tending to ½‖f*‖² at their boundary) to a critical

absorb the second moment; the third, w₁w₂(w₂ − w₁)Δc³/W², vanishes for equal shares. (b) w₁φ(θ̄ + Δθ/2) + w₂φ(θ̄ − Δθ/2) =
