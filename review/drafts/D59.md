`rich_transit_nd`; the widths are not constrained — the ends below report widths below the samples' spacing and of negative sign).

(i) *(one feature, in the continuum: no spurious minimum)* For a Gaussian feature Aφ(·; c*, σ*), the one-kernel energy with the coefficient
at its least-squares value,

    J(c, σ) = ½‖f*‖² − ½⟨f*, φ_{c,σ}⟩²/‖φ_{c,σ}‖² = ½A²π^{1/2}(σ* − 2σσ*²e^{−(c−c*)²/v}/v),   v = σ² + σ*²,

has one critical point, its global minimum J(c*, σ*) = 0, and tends to ½‖f*‖² as σ → 0, σ → ∞ or |c − c*| → ∞. So the transit's first block
on one Gaussian feature relaxes onto it exactly, whatever the width ratio, from any start whose coefficient has the feature's sign (on the
critical curve — the path's, the mass zero — a coefficient keeps its sign): one kernel, where fixed widths need several (D‴.58's quadratures
for wider features, stiff blocks for narrower ones). On samples J is J_N, whose limits as σ → 0 or ∞ fall below ½⟨f*²⟩ (a width shrinking
onto one sample, or growing past the window), so the statement is the continuum's; on the samples it is checked through the paths (A).

(ii) *(several features)* On C's random three-feature targets — separations Δ ≥ 0.4 or 0.6, widths in [0.05, 0.3], amplitudes of both signs

σ_j − σ_i = (w_j − w_i)Δc²/(6Wσ) (W = w_i + w_j) the third — an expansion in |w_i − w_j|Δc/(|W|σ), valid while |w_i − w_j|Δc ≪ |W|σ: a
