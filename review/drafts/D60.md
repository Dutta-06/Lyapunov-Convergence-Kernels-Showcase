**Proposition D‴.60 (measurement noise and the formed kernels: the adaptation's oscillator, the exact second order, and ejection).** In
D‴.49's closed loop — ẋ = f(x) + u, u = ẋ_m − k_eẽ − f̂(x̃), ẽ = x̃ − x_m, x̃ = x + n, the adaptation's regressor evaluated at x_m — let
the formed kernels sit at the exact fit (each feature's kernel at its centre with its amplitude, the others at α = 0) and let the
measurement noise be n = sν, ν a sum of tones of unit rms. On the free law's curves the formed kernels move by ẇ_i = μ_iẽφ_i and ċ_i =
v_iẽ∂_cφ_i (φ_i, ∂_cφ_i at x_m), μ_i the coefficient's mobility and v_i the centre's: v_i = Gβ_ia_i = Gmw_i under the paradigm's metric,
v_i = G·a_i under the Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao metric.

(i) *(the linear response: an oscillator)* To first order in s the loop is its linearisation along the orbit at the fit. With K(t) =
Σ_i[μ_iφ_i² + v_iw_i(∂_cφ_i)²] at x_m(t), the fit's perturbation at the reference, y = Σ_i(φ_iδw_i + w_i∂_cφ_iδc_i), obeys —
quasi-statically in the orbit —

    y″ + k_eẏ + K(t)y = K(t)(ṅ − f̂′n),   δw_i = (μ_iφ_i/K)y,   δc_i = (v_i∂_cφ_i/K)y,

a damped oscillator whose frequency √K(t) sweeps along the orbit; a tone near the top of the sweep is amplified. Under the paradigm's
metric the centres' term dominates K; while the orbit passes c_i ± σ, each kernel's own frequency peaks at √K_i =
(Gm)^{1/2}|w_i|/(σe^{1/2}) (the largest (∂_cφ_i)², 1/(σ²e), up to the small μ_iφ_i²), so √K_max ∝ (Gm)^{1/2}, and √K_max lies below a band
[ω_lo, ω_hi] of the noise when m ≲ ω_lo²/(G max_t Σ_iw_i²(∂_cφ_i)²).

(ii) *(the second order: period means)* Under the noise sν and −sν the loop's period means split into an even part, s²⟨θ₂⟩ + O(s⁴), and an
odd part, s⟨θ₁⟩ + O(s³), on any finite horizon, ⟨θ_k⟩ the period means of the loop expanded about the fit along the orbit (the second order
driven by the first through the plant's and the control's curvature, the control's slope moved by the jitter, the blur ½θ₁ᵀ∇²_θf̂θ₁, and
the regressors' and mobilities' correlation with the drive — the sources of D‴.31(iv)(c) and D‴.33(i)). The linearised loop is T-periodic
(T = 2π/ω_ref), so a tone ω_k contributes to the period means increments that rotate by ω_kT from one period to the next: bounded, by about
1/|sin(ω_kT/2)|, unless ω_k is a multiple of ω_ref, where they add up secularly. The first order's means are such increments (at
half-integer tones they alternate in sign); the second order's forcing has a constant part — the tone averages, whose response is the
averaged loop's — and parts at 2ω_k and ω_j ± ω_k, whose responses are again rotating increments. So the averaged loop reproduces ⟨θ₂⟩
where none of 2ω_k, ω_j ± ω_k is near a multiple of ω_ref after the orbit's modulation and K(t) does not cross ω_k² transversally; ⟨θ₂⟩
itself is the response of the slow coefficient mode to the constant part: a second-order offset reached with that mode's time constant, not
a drift without bound.

(iii) *(ejection; numerical)* A formed kernel leaves its feature by an ejection — a first-passage event of multi-tone noise, after hundreds
of periods —, not through (ii)'s offset: single tones and tone pairs at the same levels do not eject. Its level falls with the centres'
mobility Gmw and with resonance — a noise band covering √K_max lowers it, a band above √K_max raises it —, and rises with k_e (the
oscillator's damping, and D‴.33(iv)'s rectification, whose sign k_e reverses); under the Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao metric no ejection occurs
at the levels tried. Keeping √K_max below the noise band (m ≲ ω_lo²/(G max_t Σ_iw_i²(∂_cφ_i)²)), or the WFR metric, removes the resonance;
it is not sufficient for no ejection — the mobility Gmw matters on its own.

*Proof.* (i) With e = x − x_m the true error and d = e + n the measured one, the loop's right-hand side is ė = f(x_m + e) − f̂(x_m + d) −

w_i∂_cφ_ic₁_i) − k_ed₁, ẇ₁_i = μ_iφ_id₁, ċ₁_i = v_i∂_cφ_id₁, with y = Σ_i(φ_iw₁_i + w_i∂_cφ_ic₁_i) the fit's perturbation at x_m. Then ẏ =
Kd₁ + (the terms from φ_i, ∂_cφ_i moving along the orbit) and ḋ₁ = ṅ + ė₁ = ṅ − f′n − y − k_ed₁; dropping the orbit's terms
(quasi-statically), ÿ = Kḋ₁ = K(ṅ − f′n) − Ky − k_eẏ, and δw_i, δc_i integrate μ_iφ_id₁ and v_i∂_cφ_id₁, i.e. (μ_iφ_i/K)y and
(v_i∂_cφ_i/K)y. Under the paradigm's metric v_iw_i = Gmw_i², so K = Σ_iμ_iφ_i² + GmΣ_iw_i²(∂_cφ_i)², and (∂_cφ_i)² = ((x −
c_i)²/σ⁴)e^{−(x−c_i)²/σ²} is largest, 1/(σ²e), at x = c_i ± σ; the bound on m is √K_max ≤ ω_lo with the second sum alone. (ii) The
right-hand side is analytic in the state and in n, and the fit is a solution at n = 0, so on a finite horizon the solution expands
regularly in s: X = X₀ + sX₁ + s²X₂ + O(s³), X₁ the linearisation's response to ν and X₂ the linearisation's response to the quadratic

order. (iii) numerical. ∎
