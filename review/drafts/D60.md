**Proposition D‴.60 (measurement noise and the formed kernels: the adaptation's resonance and the noise's drift).** In D‴.49's closed
loop — ẋ = f(x) + u, u = ẋ_m − k_eẽ − f̂(x̃), ẽ = x̃ − x_m, x̃ = x + n, the adaptation's regressor evaluated at x_m — let the formed
kernels sit at the exact fit (each feature's kernel at its centre with its amplitude, the others at α = 0) and let the measurement
noise be n = sν, ν a sum of tones of unit rms. On the free law's curves the formed kernels move by ẇ_i = μ_iẽφ_i and ċ_i = v_iẽ∂_cφ_i
(φ_i, ∂_cφ_i at x_m), μ_i the coefficient's mobility and v_i the centre's: v_i = Gβ_ia_i = Gmw_i under the paradigm's metric,
v_i = G·a_i under the Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao metric.

(i) *(the linear response: an oscillator)* To first order in s the loop is its linearisation along the orbit at the fit. With
K(t) = Σ_i[μ_iφ_i² + v_iw_i(∂_cφ_i)²] at x_m(t), the fit's perturbation at the reference, y = Σ_i(φ_iδw_i + w_i∂_cφ_iδc_i), obeys —
quasi-statically in the orbit —

    y″ + k_eẏ + K(t)y = K(t)(ṅ − f̂′n),   δw_i = (μ_iφ_i/K)y,   δc_i = (v_i∂_cφ_i/K)y,

a damped oscillator whose frequency √K(t) sweeps from 0 to √K_max along the orbit; a tone near √K_max is amplified. Under the
paradigm's metric the centres' term dominates K, so √K_max grows like (Gm)^{1/2}.

(ii) *(the drift: the second order)* Under the noise sν and −sν the loop's period means split into an even part — the drift — equal
to s²⟨θ₂⟩ + O(s⁴), with ⟨θ₂⟩ the period means of the loop expanded about the fit to second order along the orbit (the first order
driving the second through the plant's and the control's curvature, the control's slope moved by the jitter, the blur
½θ₁ᵀ∇²_θf̂θ₁, and the regressors' and mobilities' correlation with the drive), which grows with time, and an odd part
s⟨θ₁⟩ + O(s³), a bounded offset left by the noise's start. Averaging the quadratic terms over the fast tones (the mean state with the
first order's complex envelopes, exact along the orbit) reproduces ⟨θ₂⟩ only where the envelopes vary slowly against the tones: away
from √K(t), and for tones that do not beat slowly against each other.

(iii) *(the metric and the thresholds)* Under the paradigm's metric the centres' mobility Gmw puts √K_max inside a noise band at the
loop's scale (here √K_max = 15.76 in 10–30 rad/s): the centres' jitter and the drift resonate. Under the Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao
metric, or with m small enough that √K_max lies below the band, the jitter is attenuated and the drift is smaller. The noise level at
which a formed kernel leaves its feature rises accordingly, and rises with k_e, the oscillator's damping. Design rule: keep √K_max
below the noise band — m ≲ ω_lo²/(G max_t Σ_iw_i²(∂_cφ_i)²) under the paradigm's metric, or the WFR metric — or raise k_e.

*Proof.* (i) With e = x − x_m the true error and d = e + n the measured one, the loop's right-hand side is ė = f(x_m + e) −
f̂(x_m + d) − k_ed, ẇ_i = μ_i(w_i)φ_i(x_m; c_i)d, ċ_i = v_i(w_i)∂_cφ_i(x_m; c_i)d. At the fit f̂ = f, so the first order is
ė₁ = −f′n − Σ_i(φ_iw₁_i + w_i∂_cφ_ic₁_i) − k_ed₁, ẇ₁_i = μ_iφ_id₁, ċ₁_i = v_i∂_cφ_id₁, with y = Σ_i(φ_iw₁_i + w_i∂_cφ_ic₁_i) the
fit's perturbation at x_m. Then ẏ = Kd₁ + (the terms from φ_i, ∂_cφ_i moving along the orbit) and ḋ₁ = ṅ + ė₁ = ṅ − f′n − y − k_ed₁;
dropping the orbit's terms (quasi-statically), ÿ = Kḋ₁ = K(ṅ − f′n) − Ky − k_eẏ, and δw_i, δc_i integrate μ_iφ_id₁ and
v_i∂_cφ_id₁, i.e. (μ_iφ_i/K)y and (v_i∂_cφ_i/K)y. Under the paradigm's metric v_iw_i = Gmw_i², so K = Σ_iμ_iφ_i² +
GmΣ_iw_i²(∂_cφ_i)², the second sum dominating here (μ_iφ_i² ≤ 0.09, against K_max = 15.76² ≈ 248, ∂_cφ_i being of order 1/σ). (ii) The right-hand side is analytic in
the state and in n, and the fit is a solution at n = 0, so on a finite horizon the solution expands regularly in s:
X = X₀ + sX₁ + s²X₂ + O(s³), X₁ the linearisation's response to ν and X₂ the linearisation's response to the quadratic terms in X₁ and ν
(the terms listed in (ii)); n → −n flips the odd orders, so the half-sum of the two runs is X₀ + s²X₂ + O(s⁴) and the half-difference
sX₁ + O(s³). Writing X₁ = Re Σ_k Z_k e^{iω_kt}, the mean of a quadratic term is ½Re Σ_k A_kB̄_k when the envelopes are constant over a
tone's period and the tones do not beat; the averaged system keeps exactly these averages (Krylov–Bogoliubov), so its error is of the
order of the envelopes' rate of change against ω_k — large where K(t) sweeps through ω_k² (the oscillator's envelope there changes at
the sweep's rate and at k_e/2) and for the cross terms of tones whose differences are slow. (iii) numerical. ∎
