**Proposition D‴.52 (close features: the transit counts two features a distance Δ apart after a delay ∝ Δ⁻² — a number-detection
statistic at the optimal order, its null law first order in the noise — and its two-kernel block places them along modes stiff as Δ²
and Δ⁶, at the Cramér–Rao bound).**
In the setting of Proposition D‴.48(iii) (the sliding transit of Gaussian kernels of width σ on a dictionary, equal masses), let

(i) *(the second entry)* If A ≠ 0, let c̄ = (A₁c₁ + A₂c₂)/A, V = A₁A₂Δ²/A and T₃ = A₁A₂(A₁ − A₂)Δ³/A² (the pair's moments about its
centroid), so that f* = Aφ(c̄) + ½V∂²_cφ(c̄) + ⅙T₃∂³_cφ(c̄) + O(|A₁A₂|Δ⁴|1 − 3A₁A₂/A²|/|A|) (the fourth moment's factor). Let the first
entrant j₁ — the kernel maximising |⟨f*φ_j⟩|, τ₁ = 1/|⟨f*φ_{j₁}⟩| — be unique and the second entrant's margin bounded away from 0 (no
tie: at a tie two kernels enter at τ₁ and Δτ₂ = 0). The first entrant's relaxation leaves the residual r₁ = ½Vψ + O(T₃),
ψ = ∂²_cφ(c̄) − (⟨∂²_cφ(c̄)φ(c̄)⟩/⟨φ(c̄)²⟩)φ(c̄) — in the continuum ⟨ψ(c̄)φ(c̄ + d)⟩ = G(d)d²/(4σ⁴) ≥ 0, G ∝ e^{−d²/(4σ²)} the kernels'

    Δτ₂ = min_j (s_j − u_j)/v_j · (1 + O(ϑ) + O((Δ/σ)²(1 + |A₁A₂|/A²))) ∝ Δ⁻²,   s_j = sign v_j,   ϑ = |A₁ − A₂|Δ/(|A|σ)

remainder is O(ϑ²) —; for a same-sign pair at the wall of A's sign (in general the first entry's runner-up), for an opposite-sign pair
at the other wall (a kernel on the pair's flank, with the minority's sign), the margin then 2 − μ instead of μ. The constant
Δτ₂Δ² = μ_{j₂}/(½(V/Δ²)⟨ψφ_{j₂}⟩) depends on the centroid's offset from the dictionary's grid through the margin μ_{j₂} and the overlap,
and vanishes at a tie. If A = 0 (a dipole, A₂ = −A₁), f* = −A₁Δ∂_cφ(c_m) + O(Δ³) (c_m the midpoint): the transit is the derivative
shape's −A₁∂_cφ(c_m) slowed by 1/Δ — τ₁, Δτ₂ ∝ Δ⁻¹, the same kernels.

(ii) *(the block)* At the exact fit the Gauss–Newton Hessian JᵀJ of ½⟨(f* − Φ(c)w)²⟩ in (w, c) has two eigenvalues of order 1 and
    λ_sep = 4Δ²⟨(P⊥e₂)²⟩·w₁²w₂²/(w₁² + w₂²)·(1 + O(Δ)),   λ_split = (Δ⁶/8)⟨(P⊥e₃)²⟩·(1 + O(Δ)),
e_n = ∂ⁿ_cφ/n! at the pair and P⊥ the projection orthogonal to the lower e_m — the split's stiffness independent of the amplitudes. The
separation direction (0, 0, 1/w₁, −1/w₂) moves the centres apart and leaves Jv = −2Δe₂ + O(Δ²); the split direction
(1, −1, Δ/(2w₁), Δ/(2w₂)) moves weight from one kernel to the other with the centres following, cancels the residual's orders 0, 1, 2
and leaves Jv = (Δ³/2)e₃ + O(Δ⁴) — the confluent Vandermonde structure of the moment map (w₁, w₂, c₁, c₂) ↦ (M₀, …, M₃), whose
singular values are of orders 1, 1, Δ, Δ³ (its determinant w₁w₂Δ⁴ their product). In the continuum ⟨(P⊥e₂)²⟩ = √π/(8σ³ℓ),
⟨(P⊥e₃)²⟩ = √π/(48σ⁵ℓ), ℓ = Nh the samples' span (N samples a spacing h apart). The fast flow on the critical curve is, in (w, c), the
gradient flow of the fit with the mobilities μ_k = w′(α_k)·2Gc tanh²(α_k/a) on the amplitudes (≈ 4Gc|w_k|/a on the exponential branch:
small) and Gm on the centres; it relaxes the split at the rate

    r_split = λ_split·2/(1/μ₁ + 1/μ₂)·(1 + O(Δ)) ≈ (Gc/a)Δ⁶⟨(P⊥e₃)²⟩|w₁w₂|/(|w₁| + |w₂|),

the split's Rayleigh quotient in the mobilities' metric, and the separation in two regimes: while it outpaces the amplitudes' mode
μ_k⟨φ_k²⟩ (the amplitudes frozen on its time scale; here Δ ≳ 0.01) at r_sep = Gm·4Δ²⟨(P_{⊥e₁}e₂)²⟩w₁²w₂²/(w₁² + w₂²), the projection
orthogonal to the translation only (in the continuum ⟨(P_{⊥e₁}e₂)²⟩ = 3√π/(16σ³ℓ), 3/2 of ⟨(P⊥e₂)²⟩) — the frozen amplitudes' error
O(μ⟨φ²⟩/r_sep) growing like Δ⁻² toward the crossover —, and once slower than the amplitudes (Δ ≲ 0.002 here) at Gm·λ_sep, 2/3 of
that, the amplitudes following. The sliding path's relaxation crawls along the split, which a least-squares polish of the block reaches
at once (`polish_block`).

(iii) *(counting under noise: the second knot's law)* With labels y = f* + ξ, ξ i.i.d. N(0, s²) on the N samples, the first fit's
residual is r₁ = ½Vψ + Rξ to first order in the noise — R the projection off the first fit's tangent span(φ, w∂_cφ) — so after the first
entry the duals move at
    v_j = ½V⟨ψφ_j⟩ + n_j,   n ~ N(0, s²ΦᵀRΦ/N²),
the noise's rates projected (for the kernels next to the fit Rφ_j is a small part of φ_j), and the second knot is
Δτ₂ = min_j (1 − sign(v_j)u_j)/|v_j| over the whole dictionary (u_j the duals at τ₁, noisy too). Under one feature (H0: the closest
single bump, V = 0) this is the knot's null law, exact to first order in the noise: a delay ∝ √N/s with a heavy tail, its median set by
far kernels and its lower quantiles by the fit's neighbours, whose margins and projected rates depend on the centroid's offset from
the grid — so the null quantile moves by an order of magnitude within one grid cell, and a threshold set at one offset is miscalibrated
at another. Under two features the knot comes after ≈ (i)'s deterministic Δτ₂. The power is ½ where ½|V|⟨ψφ_{j₂}⟩ ≈
z_{1−α}s(⟨(Rφ_{j₂})²⟩/N)^{1/2}, i.e. Δ² ∝ (|A|/|A₁A₂|)s/√N — the number-detection order s ∝ Δ² of Shahram–Milanfar's statistical
resolution limit and of Liu–Zhang's (σ/m)^{1/(2n−2)} at n = 2. The runner-up's projected kernel Rφ_{j₂} is nearly parallel to ψ, the
shape of the pair's deviation from one bump, so its dual is nearly that deviation's matched filter: the power is close to the
Neyman–Pearson bound Φ(‖f₁ − f₀‖/s − z_{1−α}) of the simple hypotheses. As a procedure the knot is calibrated by its law at the data's
own first fit (ĉ, ŵ), a parametric bootstrap, which keeps its size across the grid offset, where a threshold fixed at one offset
does not, at nearly the oracle-calibrated test's power ((E)).

(iv) *(placing under noise)* The block's fit after the second entry has position errors ∝ s/Δ² and amplitude-split errors ∝ s/Δ³ (the
inverse singular values of (ii): the Δ⁶ mode is mostly the split, which moves the centres by O(Δ) per unit) — the minimax orders of a
cluster of two (Batenkov–Goldman–Yomdin: SRF^{2ℓ−2}, SRF^{2ℓ−1}) — and attains the Cramér–Rao bound s²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ while it resolves the
pair. The split is lost once its bound reaches the split's scale, s ≈ 15Δ³ (the bound's std 0.1) — the support-recovery order
(Liu–Zhang's (σ/m)^{1/(2n−1)}; BLASSO's Δ^{2N−1}, Denoyelle–Duval–Peyré) —; the positions jump once the second kernel fits the noise
instead of the second feature.
