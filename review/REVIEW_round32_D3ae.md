### Verdicts
- **(i) The formula is right; "proved" overreaches.**
  - Every factor in the metric, in Π_D and in s checks out.
  - At σ = 0.1 the 64-path means follow the claimed flow: fresh 0.003–0.009, AR(1) 0.006–0.035. Flows with the w-drift doubled or halved miss by 0.13–0.59. B's 0.32 deviation was 8-path noise.
  - D41(iv) needs a spectral gap in the normal directions. The whole end phase of the selection lies outside that hypothesis: fewer than N atoms alive, λ_min(K) → 0, and M singular there.
  - At σ ≥ 0.3 the outcome of label-noise descent departs from the flow.
- **(ii) True only in the limit h → 0, σ → 0.**
  - In that limit the white flow reaches BP, even from P₀ = 10⁻⁶ and from starts whose signs must flip.
  - At the experiments' step h = 0.5 the white selection is Damian–Ma–Lee's strictly convex Ψ, and its end is not BP. At finite σ the descent kills atoms, as in a Lasso.
  - The convergence proof in the degenerate metric remains a sketch, as the status says.
- **(iii) The identities are right; the rest is loose.**
  - Cauchy–Binet, the concavity and the support count hold.
  - "Global minimisers are the sparsest fits" holds only asymptotically.
  - The identification with SBL is loose, and the claim about the weights is wrong in detail.
- **(iv) True in the continuum, false at the step the experiments use.** It fails for ρ < 1/3 and for hλ ≳ 1. "Only white noise gives a convex selection" is false at a finite step.
- **(v) The expansion and its sufficiency are right; the rest overreaches.**
  - The minimal-norm certificate is very conservative.
  - "However coherent the dictionary" hides a threshold in τ_c that grows without bound with the coherence.
  - The parenthetical about the spark is wrong.
  - The dead-atom remark is true but trivial.
- **Numerics.**
  - A: right, apart from one transcription slip.
  - B: recovery depends on the seed and on σ.
  - C: robust. It is insensitive to the threshold, the flows have converged, and it persists under the other magnitude laws and at 40 × 160. But plain SBL matches or beats it.
  - D: one number misquoted and instance 13 misread.
  - E: the flows have converged.
  - F: the α = 2 result is a horizon artefact.
  - G: the reweighted-ℓ¹ baseline is unfair.
  - H: run on Gaussian designs only.
- **Mechanism.** The selection is an O(h) effect of the explicit Euler step.
- **Literature.** I found no prior statement that temporally coloured label noise induces a log-det, SBL-like or ℓ0-like bias (6 searches). But Wipf–Nagarajan's Theorem 2 already proves the "recovers where ℓ¹ fails" phenomenon for an SBL-type non-separable reweighting.

### Lesson
The headline "slow label noise beats basis pursuit" holds at the level of the reduced flow. It is robust to the threshold and the magnitude law, it holds at 40 × 160, and the flow's formula is right. But the review exposed everything around it. One "never" was an integration horizon (D3ae-1). The 4/4 seeds were luck: 4 more gave 0, 0, 0, 2/16 (D3ae-3). The noisy agreement breaks by σ = 0.3 (D3ae-2). The "white = basis pursuit" comparator is not basis pursuit at the step actually used (D3ae-5). The whole effect is an Euler-step O(h) term that Heun suppresses 25× (D3ae-6). And the classical method that implements the same log-det penalty, SBL, which was left out, matches or beats it (D3ae-4). For a striking effect: benchmark it against the classical method that minimises the same penalty, rerun every "all" or "never" with a convergence criterion, more seeds and a σ sweep, and label any selection derived from a discrete scheme's Itô drift as such in the README and audit, not only in the statement.
