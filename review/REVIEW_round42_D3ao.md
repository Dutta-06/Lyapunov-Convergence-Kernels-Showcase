## Referee D3ao, round 42: Proposition D‴.54 (the transit in ℝⁿ)

The two largest problems both come from one assumption. The split at a near tie, and with it the threshold x_θ, depends on where the feature sits along the Voronoi face, not only on the gaps. Ignoring this:
- mis-states the formation rule (iv) and E[N−1];
- makes the reported "1.6·10⁻² failure of factorisation" an artefact of the reference used (the true error is 3.5·10⁻⁴);
- biases P(N≥3) and P(N=4) upward by 8–9%.

The Voronoi selection (ii) fails at the sample window's edges, with unequal masses and on non-tensor sampling. The ℤⁿ codimension law is non-generic. The proof status of (i)/(iii) is overstated. One positive result: the n-D creep law in (i) is correct (I verified it; the draft's Part C did not).

### Verdicts
- **(i) Dimension-free.** No step of D‴.48(iii)(a), D‴.51 or D‴.53 really needs n = 1:
  - Lemma D‴.53a's one-dimensional normalisation is cosmetic.
  - For k = 2, collinearity reduces the critical points to the 1-D case.
  - The creep law holds as a vector law. On target 0, |disp − εC_j|/|εC_j| ≤ 3.6·10⁻² at 10⁻⁸ and 3.6·10⁻³ at 10⁻¹⁰, with cos ≥ 0.99998 (my check).

  But D‴.48(iii)(b) is only formal, and D‴.53 assumes simple entries (H1) and non-degenerate blocks (H2), which every near tie violates. Part C is inadequate: six exactly representable, interior, equal-mass targets 3.6–7σ apart, none with a near tie. It tests none of D‴.53's rate along the path, D‴.53(c), D‴.53(d) or D‴.51(iv); no n-D code exists for those.
- **(ii) Voronoi selection.**
  - Exact in the continuum. In the interior, the grid's error is ≤ 6·10⁻⁸.
  - False near the sample window's edge, with unequal masses (it becomes a power diagram), on coarse or anisotropic grids, and in multi-feature targets. The margin formula holds only for equal masses in the continuum.
- **(iii) The k-fold near tie.**
  - The loop does converge to the k-kernel inner problem: successive differences shrink by 3.13–3.19 per decade, close to √10. This is not proved for k ≥ 2 kernels in the inner layer.
  - The Łojasiewicz proof has gaps (detailed in D3ao-10).
  - The shares 𝒮 depend on the feature's position on the face, not only on x.
  - (a) Correct; the experiment's sample grid does have the symmetry.
  - (b) Correct only with the feature at the common centre. The softmax error bound is false.
  - (c) Partly mis-explained.
  - (d) Additivity is exact on tensor grids. The factorisation is far better than claimed (3.5·10⁻⁴ in 2-D, ≤ 1·10⁻³ in 3-D) once the edge factors use the vertex's geometry; the stated 1.6·10⁻² is an artefact.
- **(iv) Formation rule and probabilities.**
  - x_θ must be a function on the face.
  - P(N≥3) and P(N=4) are overstated by 9% and 8%.
  - The ℤⁿ codimension law needs exact multiplicativity and range limits.
  - The hexagonal claim is checked nowhere in the draft. My check: c(3) = 2 holds, P(N≥3) ≈ 2.4·10³ε, and N = 4 never occurs.
  - Part D cannot resolve the exponent of P(N≥3).
  - The mass formula ignores the creep.
- **Numerics paragraph.** Mostly faithful to the md/raw, with the specific exceptions in D3ao-9, -12 and -14.

### Lesson
The draft treats its reference objects as exact and reads their errors as properties of the dynamics. The edge problem was posed on the kernels' axis and then used as a factor at a vertex where the feature sits off that axis. The inner problem at a finite start xi0 was used as the ε → 0 limit. The result was a reported "1.6% failure of factorisation", three phantom boundary points, P(N≥3) and P(N=4) 8–9% high, and a last-decade convergence that looks faster than ε^{1/2}. The deeper miss is that in n dimensions the one-dimensional constant x_θ becomes a function on the face. The extra dimensions are exactly where the new behaviour and the new errors appear. Before attributing a discrepancy to the phenomenon, vary the reference's own parameters (offset, xi0, sampling, masses, edges) and confirm the reference is the limit of what it is compared against.
