## Confirmed issues

**D3-1 (minor — test/numerics premise). Hypothesis (H) fails for the ϱ′ = 0.3 that test 5 passes to `theorem_constants`.**
On the test's warm start at s = 40 (seed 11, M = 512), a dense search (400 Fibonacci directions × 4 radii) over the G⁻¹-neighbourhood of the reference support gives
sup κ_R = 0.00881 (atoms), 0.00956 (ϱ′ = 0.05), 0.01052 (0.1), 0.01248 (0.2), **0.01451 (0.3) > σ₀ = 0.014**. The test asserts only `kR_ref.max() < σ₀` at the atoms (0.0095) and sets κ := σ₀ − max_atoms κ_R = 0.0045, then calls `theorem_constants(..., rho_prime=0.3)`: those are not the theorem's constants for that ϱ′ (κ would be negative). The margin is roughly linear in ϱ′ (slope ≈ 0.019), so (H) holds here only for ϱ′ ≲ 0.27.
*Fix:* compute κ from the sup over N_ϱ′ with the ϱ′ actually used (e.g. ϱ′ = 0.1 → κ ≈ 0.0035), and say so in the test docstring and in the numerics paragraph.

**D3-2 (minor — code). `kappa_R_neighbourhood` is a Monte Carlo lower estimate and under-estimates the sup.** With `n_dirs=4` it returns 0.01249 and with 64 directions 0.01311 against the dense 0.01451 (ϱ′ = 0.3, same state). The docstring admits "from below", but `coupled_run` records it as `kR_ref_nbhd` and the numerics will read it as κ_R[μ_s](N_ϱ′). A lower estimate cannot verify (H). In every check the sup sat on the boundary sphere (rad = ϱ′).
*Fix:* deterministic search on the sphere of radius ϱ′ (≥ 400 Fibonacci directions, plus a few inner radii) and report it as an estimate; or bound κ_R(N_ϱ′) ≤ κ_R(supp) + ϱ′·L₃^G⟨|r|⟩ with L₃^G the whitened third-derivative bound.

**D3-3 (minor — vacuous test, misleading name). `test_warm_start_coupled_run_contracts_and_stays_below_the_bound`.**
(i) `D.max() < c['C1']/m` compares D = 0.22 with C₁/m = 3.2·10⁶ (C₁ = 5.2·10⁷; also R_Θ = 1444, D_max = 8.3·10⁶, ϱ = 2.1·10⁻⁵, θ = 5.7·10⁻⁹, Δ = 8.8·10⁻¹², m₀ = 7.8·10¹⁸): it cannot fail.
(ii) "contracts": D is monotonically increasing over the whole run for both m (m = 16: 2.8·10⁻⁷ → 0.221; m = 64: 3.8·10⁻⁷ → 0.135; every increment positive). The ratio D₁₆/D₆₄ is 0.73 at s = 0.02, 1.03 at s = 10 and 1.64 at s = 40, against 4.43 predicted by the sampling variance (1/m − 1/M): the m-law is not visible on this horizon; the final assertion `D[64].max() < D[16].max()` holds with margin 1.6 only. (The contraction rate a ≈ 0.005 has time scale ≈ 200 ≫ 40; the run is Theorem D′'s transient.)
(iii) The (c)-check `hnorm_mf² ≤ 2L²D + 2·16(1/m + 1/M)`: the stand-in's fluctuation has E Y_M² = ⟨Var_μ Φ̃⟩(1/m − 1/M) = 0.056 (measured 0.0575) while the test's 16(1/m + 1/M) = 1.03 — 18× slack, and the sign of the 1/M term is wrong (it is −1/M because the m-system shares its particles with the reference, as §5c's numerics note).
(iv) At s = 40, 9/16 (resp. 19/64) particles sit farther than ϱ′ = 0.3 from their references (max ‖δ_i‖_{G⁻¹} = 0.94, resp. 0.68) — no contradiction (m ≪ m₀), but "stays below the bound" is empty.
*Fix:* rename; replace (i) by a check of the mechanism along the run (e.g. the recorded decomposition: T_A ≤ −(a − L₂^GY)D and T_B ≤ −‖h‖² + (L₂^G/2)D‖h‖ at every record), state the stand-in variance as (1/m − 1/M)⟨Var⟩, and if an m-law is asserted, use a longer horizon or a final-time ratio with a tolerance.

**D3-6 (minor — code bug, anti-conservative). `Lambda1_from_profile` truncates ∫₀^{s₁} at the last grid point ≤ s₁.** On s = 0, 1, …, 10 with (κ − a)₊ ≡ 0.01 and s₁ = 5.5 it returns 0.050 instead of 0.055. **D3-7 (cosmetic — code). `L_2_G_closed = λ_G·a_max/s_min²` is valid but loose by λ_G/G_c.** 300-start Nelder–Mead on the analytic Hessian: sup‖∇²Φ̃‖ (unwhitened) = 44.444444 = a_max/s_min² (confirms the §5c closed form), and the whitened sup is G_c·a_max/s_min² for every G tested (I, (1, .25, .25), (4, 1, 1), (.25, 1, .25)): 11.1111 against the code's 44.44 for the test's G. **D3-8 (cosmetic — docstring).** The module docstring states T_A ≤ −(σ₀ − κ_R)D without the L₂^GY correction of (2.1); `decomposition`'s docstring says "(the mean-field drift at ϑ̄_i uses the residual f − fhat_mf)" — correct, but note that with `fhat_mf=None` the reference is the empirical f̂_Θ̄ and T_C ≡ 0 by construction (which is what test 2 relies on).

**D3-9 (cosmetic — block, Steps 6–8 and (c)).** 𝒢 is defined with sup_{s≥0}, but (a) bounds P(sup_{s≤S}Y > θ) and Step 7 uses D ≤ Z "on 𝒢". **D3-10 (cosmetic — block, Setting).** "(a Vlasov equation with a bounded vector field that is Lipschitz in ϑ …)": the field −G∇𝒱[μ] = G⟨r_μ∇Φ̃⟩ − σ₀(ϑ − ϑ⁰) is unbounded (linear leak); it is bounded on the invariant ball ‖ϑ − ϑ⁰‖_{G⁻¹} ≤ R_Θ and of linear growth — Dobrushin's theorem in its bounded-force form does not apply verbatim; the standard McKean–Vlasov well-posedness with globally Lipschitz, linearly growing coefficients does. Also the garbled fragment "so that ∇²_ϑ𝒱[μ](ϑ) = G⁻¹ − ... precisely G^{1/2}…" should be cleaned up.

**D3-11 (minor — Remark D‴.2(ii) and the Status paragraph).** "on a ridge 𝒱 has a direction of negative curvature, κ_R > σ₀ — (H) fails for every s" is false pointwise. Counterexample: 𝒱(x,y) = (x² − 1)² + y² + 10x²y² has the invariant separatrix {x = 0} (∂_x𝒱 = 4x(x² − 1) + 20xy² vanishes there), on which ∇²𝒱 = diag(20y² − 4, 2) is positive definite for |y| > 0.45; a connected support crossing the separatrix there contains no point of negative curvature at that time. What is true: the filament's points flow along the separatrix to critical points of 𝒱[μ_∞] that are not minima (saddles, under a Morse assumption), where λ_max(R^G) > σ₀, so κ_R[μ_s](supp μ_s) > σ₀ for all large s and **(H) fails for every s₁**. Replace "for every s" by "for all s large enough / for every s₁" and "on a ridge … negative curvature" by "at the saddles the filament converges to".

**D3-12 (cosmetic — Remark D‴.1).** "which every Wasserstein-stable stationary measure satisfies in its non-strict form (an atom at a point where 𝒱[μ_∞] is not locally minimal is unstable)": the splitting argument (move half of the atom's mass by ±εu along a direction of negative curvature: first variation mass·ε²⟨u, ∇²𝒱u⟩/2 < 0, quadratic term O(mass²ε⁴)) needs an atom of positive mass and yields "not a local minimiser of 𝒢 in W₂", not instability of every non-atomic stationary measure. State it for atomic μ_∞ (the case at hand) or cite the Chizat–Bach optimality condition; "Wasserstein-stable" is not defined in the block.

**D3-13 (minor — numerics premise).** The test's "warm start" is not near the mean-field stationary configuration: along the run the reference's fit error grows 0.037 → 0.261, mean |a| shrinks 2.51 → 2.02, mean s grows 0.47 → 0.66 (the leak toward ϑ⁰ = 0 pulls the fitted cluster), drift_rms 0.093 → 0.040. "κ_R < σ₀ along the run" is therefore a transient observation; (H) for s ≥ s₁ (and the window of Remark D‴.1) is not verified by it. The numerics paragraph should check (H) at a near-stationary state (drift_rms ≪ σ₀·R) over the neighbourhood actually used, and exhibit one instance where it holds with ϱ′ > 0 — the current instance does not at ϱ′ = 0.3 (D3-1).

**D3-14 (cosmetic — test 2).** `assert dd['T_B'] < 0` holds for the test's configuration only; T_B > 0 is possible (adversarial search: T_B = +1.67 at D = 3.9, m = 1, a particle near saturation), consistent with (3.1) (ratio to the bound 0.014).
